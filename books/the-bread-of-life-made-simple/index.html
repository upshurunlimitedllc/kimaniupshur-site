The Bread of Life

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Faith & Doctrine

The Bread of Life — How Jesus Satisfies the Hunger Nothing Else Can — follows the bread trail through Scripture, in chapters numbered Two through Eleven as the book is printed. It begins with manna in the morning: the bread God rained on the wilderness (Exodus 16:4) and Jesus' own verdict on it in John 6:49. It hears the lesson of the manna — man does not live by bread alone (Deuteronomy 8:3; Matthew 4:4) — and then stands before the claim itself: "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35), the Father who gives the true bread (John 6:32). It does not soften the hard teaching — eat My flesh, drink My blood (John 6:53–58) — nor skip the Spirit who gives life (John 6:63), the hard sayings, and the disciples who stayed when others left (John 6:66–68). It prays for daily bread (Matthew 6:11), watches the bread broken, given, and enough at the Last Supper, and ends at the Emmaus table, where the stranger becomes the host (Luke 24). Its recurring warning is the book's own: hunger can make bondage look like a banquet, and yesterday's manna is no substitute for fresh bread. Every chapter opens with a headed Scripture quotation and closes with a Reflection question; the Conclusion is titled "The Table Is Set," and a Closing Prayer follows. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
