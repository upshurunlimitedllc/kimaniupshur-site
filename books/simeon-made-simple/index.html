Simeon

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Simeon is the story of an old man in Jerusalem who refused to stop watching. Scripture introduces him in three short verses — Luke 2:25–27 — as just and devout, "waiting for the consolation of Israel," with the Holy Ghost upon him and a promise that he would not see death before he had seen the Lord's Christ. After some four hundred years of silence since Malachi, most people had stopped expecting God to act. Simeon had not. This book walks with him from that long waiting to the day he held the child in the temple. Twelve chapters follow his song and his prophecy phrase by phrase: the quiet architecture of a righteous life, waiting without going bitter, the Spirit-led life before Pentecost, and the promise that tethered him to Jerusalem. Then comes the meeting itself — "Mine eyes have seen thy salvation" — a light to lighten the Gentiles and the glory of Israel, and the satisfied departure in peace. The final chapters face the harder edge of his words: a child set for the fall and rising of many, a sword that would pierce Mary's soul, and a Christ who reveals the thoughts of many hearts. A conclusion and a closing prayer complete the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
