Hiring Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Business & Leadership

Hiring Made Simple starts from an uncomfortable observation: a bad hire does not feel like a mistake in the moment. The candidate seemed sharp, said the right things, and had a decent resume — and only months later does the gap between how someone interviews and how they perform become visible, after the cost has already been paid. Subtitled “How to Actually Predict Who Will Succeed, Instead of Guessing and Hoping,” the book builds a hiring process designed to predict performance rather than one that merely feels thorough. Thirteen chapters work through the whole sequence: defining the job before defining the person, writing a posting that attracts the right people, screening resumes without wasting everyone's time, and structuring interviews that actually predict performance. It covers questions worth asking and ones that are not, the biases that quietly wreck good interviews, reference checks that actually tell you something, and who should actually be in the room. It then turns to making the actual decision, extending the offer and negotiating well, the real cost of a bad hire, building genuinely diverse teams without lowering the bar, and building a process you will actually reuse — so the next hire rests on deliberate structure, not an improvised conversation and a gut check. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
