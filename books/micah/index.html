Micah

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Micah — subtitled “Act Justly, Love Mercy, Walk Humbly” — is a walkthrough of the book of Micah, the country prophet from the village of Moresheth who carried God's courtroom case against His people. The How to Use This Book page sets the pace — read one chapter a day; twelve chapters, twelve days — and the method: “Scripture first, teaching follows. Every chapter opens with God's Word, and the teaching bows to it.” Readers move through the book in order, traveling the whole arc: judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem, a remnant gathered, a ruler promised from Bethlehem, the most famous sentence in the prophets, and a prayer that answers the prophet's own name. The Introduction, “Three Words from the Country Prophet,” compresses the message into three words — Hear, Hope, Walk — and describes the book as a courtroom with three cases: indictment, promise, pardon. The twelve chapters run from “The Word Came,” “The Prophet Weeps,” and “Woe to the Oppressors” through “Swords into Plowshares,” “O Bethlehem Ephrathah,” and “What Does the Lord Require?” to “Who Is a God Like You?” Each chapter ends with a section headed “Your move this week.” A Conclusion, “Three Words from the Courtroom,” and a Closing Prayer follow. On its copyright page the book states that Scripture quotations are from the ESV® Bible (The Holy Bible, English Standard Version®), and that Scripture quotations marked (KJV) are taken from the King James Version of the Bible; in the text, the long Micah passages are labeled (ESV) and shorter quotations are labeled (KJV). The back matter includes “A PERSONAL INVITATION: GIVE YOUR LIFE TO JESUS CHRIST,” with THE SINNER'S PRAYER and a “WHAT COMES NEXT?” section. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
