Timothy

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Timothy was young, and the assignment was enormous. Timothy follows the young pastor Paul left in Ephesus to charge some “that they teach no other doctrine” (1 Timothy 1:3), and asks what his road — being taught, tested, and trusted — teaches anyone who has ever felt younger than the work God gave them. The book opens with its anchor verse, “Let no man despise thy youth” (1 Timothy 4:12), and states plainly that it draws only from Acts and Paul’s two letters to Timothy, staying silent where Scripture is silent. Its thirteen chapters begin with the faith that dwelt first in his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice, and the holy Scriptures he knew from childhood (2 Timothy 1:5; 3:15). They move through the Spirit who gives power, love, and a sound mind rather than fear (2 Timothy 1:7), the deposit entrusted to him, preaching the Word, holding fast sound words, and fighting the good fight. Character receives its own attention — the overseer’s qualifications, handling false teaching, and godliness with contentment — before the loneliness of the faithful and Paul’s final charge and crown. The governing conviction throughout is stewardship: “it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful” (1 Corinthians 4:2). A Closing Prayer completes the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
