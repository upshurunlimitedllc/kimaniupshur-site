Saving Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Money & Finance

Saving Made Simple — How to Save Money on Any Income Without Feeling Deprived — starts with a simple reversal: save first, and spend what is left, instead of spending first and saving whatever survives the month. Following Marcus and Tanya, a couple who saved only $47 a month before flipping the order, the book teaches the real machinery of saving chapter by chapter: why saving is so hard, the pay-yourself-first decision, and the savings hierarchy that gives every dollar an address. It then builds the system in order — an emergency fund done right, sinking funds that kill surprise expenses, full automation, a plan for irregular income, and a guide to where money should wait, including high-yield accounts. Later chapters cover cutting spending without misery, the goal math behind big purchases, the line between saving and investing, the savings rate as the one number that matters, and a lifetime plan reviewed at an annual summit, with a one-page savings plan in the appendix and one actionable step in every chapter. Biblical wisdom runs through the mechanics — the ant of Proverbs 6:6, Joseph storing in plenty for famine, and the talents of Matthew 25 — because saving, here, is stewardship, not hoarding. It will not promise that saving is easy, and it will not blame lattes for an empty account. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
