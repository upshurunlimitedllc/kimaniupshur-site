Prompt Engineering Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Tech & AI

Prompt Engineering Made Simple treats communicating with AI as a communication skill, not a technical skill reserved for programmers. Most disappointing AI results, it argues, are not a technology problem but an input problem: the tool is capable, the instructions are unclear, and the output reflects the input. In six chapters it explains what prompt engineering is, why prompts shape AI responses, and how to write better prompts by asking clearly, giving context, and pushing back when an answer misses the point. It then applies those techniques to work and productivity and to learning and research, showing how the same instincts used in everyday conversation — explaining what you need and checking what comes back — produce better writing and better results. A final chapter covers the limitations and responsible use of AI, including work that may be better done without AI assistance or with only light assistance. A framework summary gathers the key takeaways. Examples throughout are grounded in real contexts, in line with the author's background in education and his emphasis on building understanding that remains useful as circumstances change. The book is written for anyone willing to think clearly about what they are asking for and how they are asking for it, and its central skill — thinking clearly about what you want and expressing it clearly — remains valuable however the technology changes. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
