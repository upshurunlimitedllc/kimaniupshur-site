Alaska Penal Code Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

On a January night in Fairbanks, forty below zero, a driver slides through a stop sign she never saw; a hunter in the Brooks Range dresses a moose ten feet past a boundary he never noticed; a teenager in Juneau learns that "borrowing" a car has a legal definition of its own. None of them set out to break the law — all of them broke it anyway. Alaska Penal Code Made Simple translates the law that governs those moments into plain language. It gives the law its real name — Alaska does not officially call it a penal code; criminal law lives in the Alaska Statutes, Title 11, "Criminal Law" — and maps its chapters, from offenses against the person and property to public order, weapons, and controlled substances. Twelve chapters, meant to be read one a day, explain how criminal law works in Alaska, how offenses are classified — unclassified and class A, B, and C felonies, class A and B misdemeanors, and violations — and how a case moves step by step through booking, bail and pretrial release, arraignment, plea agreements, and trial. Later chapters cover charges and penalties, key legal concepts, what happens after a conviction, the rights you should know, and the common misunderstandings the final chapter clears away. Each chapter ends with Know the Law and Protect Yourself. The book is education, not legal advice: Know. Comply. Protect. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
