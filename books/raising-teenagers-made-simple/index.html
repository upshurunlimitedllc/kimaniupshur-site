Raising Teenagers Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Family & Relationships

Raising Teenagers begins from a conviction stated up front: parenting a teenager is not control but influence, the move from manager to consultant, from commander to coach, shepherding a teenager's heart from a Christian worldview where children are arrows, not property, and parenting is stewardship, not ownership. The teenage years are not a defect but a construction project: the brain is being rebuilt, and the wiring for judgment, impulse control, and long-term thinking will not be finished until the mid-twenties. Thirteen chapters teach the craft in order. They explain what is actually happening in the teenage brain, the shift from managing behavior to shaping hearts, connection before correction, and listening that does not shut a teenager down. They build boundaries that survive contact with a teenager and discipline that teaches instead of controlling, then give a sane framework for phones, social media, and the digital world. Later chapters take up friends, dating, and peer pressure; school, grades, and expectations; what to do when they lie, defy, or fail; the big conversations about sex, drugs, faith, and doubt; holding tight versus letting go; and launching them into adulthood. The book states plainly that it is not medical, psychological, or legal advice, and that some struggles need professional help first. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
