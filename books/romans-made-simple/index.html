Romans

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Romans is a biblical guide to the gospel of God — a plain-language walk through the apostle Paul's letter to the Romans, the letter the introduction calls the one that changed the world. It opens with Romans 1:16–17, the passage that broke over Martin Luther and warmed John Wesley's heart on Aldersgate Street, and follows Paul's own staircase: the bad news first, because the gospel makes no sense without it, and then the good news in its full glory. Across fourteen chapters it traces that argument — not ashamed of the gospel, the exchange of truth for a lie, no excuse and no exception, all have sinned (Romans 3:23), justified freely by grace (Romans 3:24), Abraham's faith, peace with God, dead to sin and alive in Christ, the Spirit who sets believers free, the war inside, the God who chooses, the branches and the root, the living sacrifice, and the weak and the strong. Scripture quotations are from the New International Version (NIV). An appendix poses twelve honest questions about Romans, and the book closes with a conclusion on the righteousness of God revealed, a closing prayer, and a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ, including the Sinner's Prayer and guidance on what comes next. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
