Onesimus

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Onesimus tells the story of the runaway slave at the heart of Paul's letter to Philemon — and of the gospel that turned him from runaway to brother. Onesimus fled his master in Colossae and disappeared into Rome, where he met a chained apostle and believed. Paul then sent him back with a letter asking Philemon to receive him 'no longer as a slave but more than a slave — a beloved brother' (Philemon 1:16). The book is Scripture-first: every chapter is anchored in all twenty-five verses of Philemon, together with the passage in Colossians where Onesimus reappears as 'a faithful and beloved brother' (Colossians 4:9). Thirteen chapters trace the whole arc. They follow the runaway and the providence that used his flight, Paul becoming a father in chains, and the meaning of a name that means 'useful' — the man named Useful who had become useless, made profitable at last. They take up Paul's intercession ('Receive him as myself'), forgiveness that costs ('Put that on my account'), grace for the runaway who is not too far gone, restoration done right as a lesson for the church, Philemon's own transformation from master to brother, and the eternal weight of a short letter. The conclusion names the pattern: Runaway. Restored. Brother. It serves readers who have run — from God, from people they have wronged, from their own past — and readers who must learn to receive the restored. Its conviction is stated at the outset: nobody is beyond the reach of grace. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
