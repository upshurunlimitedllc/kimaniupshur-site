Exercise Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Health & Wellness

Exercise is one of the most researched topics on earth — and somehow one of the most confusing. Exercise names the cost of that confusion the confusion tax: years paid in intending, starting, and stopping, while a fitness industry profits from making a simple thing look complicated. Against the noise, the book stakes a modest claim: the fundamentals are settled — move most days, get your heart pumping regularly, challenge your muscles a couple of times a week, start where you are, and continue for years; that is about ninety percent of it, and the rest is optimization. Twelve chapters work the fundamentals in order: what exercise is really for; how much is enough; cardio and strength training, each made simple and each doable without a gym; a beginner's protocol; exercise for busy lives and for older bodies; the consistency machine that makes it automatic; the mental side; fuel; pain, injury, and rest; and a lifetime plan. It is not a workout program, not a weight-loss book, and not medical advice — readers with a condition or injury are pointed to their doctor. The author writes from a Christian worldview: the body is a gift entrusted to us, caring for it is stewardship rather than vanity, and exercise, done right, is gratitude expressed in motion. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
