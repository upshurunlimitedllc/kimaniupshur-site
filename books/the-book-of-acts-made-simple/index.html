The Book of Acts

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

The Book of Acts calls itself "The Book the Spirit Wrote" — the story of the Holy Spirit and the early church, built on Jesus' promise in Acts 1:8. It also notes what makes Acts unique: it is the only book of the Bible with no real ending — "You and I are chapter 29." Across fourteen chapters it walks the whole book in order: waiting for the power (Acts 1:4) among the 120 believers in the upper room; the wind and the fire at Pentecost (Acts 2); healing in the name of Jesus (Acts 3:6); the shared life of a church with nothing held back (Acts 4:32); Stephen, whose face was like the face of an angel (Acts 6:15); and the gospel scattering under persecution (Acts 8:4). It follows the hunter who became the hunted on the Damascus road (Acts 9:5), the wall coming down at Cornelius's house (Acts 10:34–35), prison doors opening (Acts 12:5), and churches sent by the Spirit (Acts 13:4). It defends grace plus nothing (Acts 15:11), preaches to Athens' unknown God (Acts 17:22–23), passes through many hardships (Acts 14:22), and ends unhindered (Acts 28:31). Every chapter opens with Scripture and closes with Your Action Step; quotations are from the NIV. An appendix answers twelve honest questions about Acts, and the Conclusion sums the book up: Filled. Sent. Multiplying. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
