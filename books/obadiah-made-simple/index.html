Obadiah

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Obadiah walks verse by verse through the shortest book in the Old Testament — twenty-one verses, one chapter, a page most readers turn too quickly on the way to Jonah. The book calls it the shortest prophecy with the surest warning: a message about pride, delivered by God through His servant to Edom, that has not lost its weight in nearly three thousand years. Its hook comes straight from verse three, in the language of the King James Version: 'The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee.' Its tagline is the whole story in four words: Pride falls. God stands. Across thirteen chapters, the book follows the prophecy in order — the servant in the storm, heaven's summons to the nations, the great reversal that makes the proud small, the clefts of the rock where pride hides, and the eagle's height that is not beyond God. It then faces Edom's betrayal and its sin against its brother, the nearness of the Day of the Lord, deliverance on Mount Zion, and a kingdom that shall be the LORD's. A conclusion, 'Choose Your Rock,' a Closing Prayer, and a Personal Invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ — with the Sinner's Prayer — close the book. Obadiah is for readers who want to see their own pride clearly — in the nation, the boardroom, the pulpit, and the mirror — and to stand on the Rock that does not fall. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
