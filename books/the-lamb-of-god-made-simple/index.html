The Lamb of God

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

"Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." The Lamb of God takes John the Baptist's cry as its thesis and reads it, in its own argument, as penal substitution and never mere moral example — then traces the Lamb from Eden's promise to the throne of worship in twelve chapters. The Lamb slain before the foundation of the world; the Lamb in the garden; Abraham on Moriah — "God will provide Himself a lamb"; the Passover lamb, whose blood must be applied: "when I see the blood, I will pass over you." The suffering servant, like a lamb to the slaughter; the blood that takes away sin, with Acts 16:31's invitation — believe on the Lord Jesus Christ; worthy is the Lamb who was slain; the wrath of the Lamb, where the book insists the sin of the world is the scope of the offer, not universalism; following the Lamb wherever He goes; and beholding Him now. It reads "It is finished" through tetelestai, the debt-paid receipt word, and holds its paradox plainly: the Lion is the Lamb — the conqueror is the sacrifice. A reflection section follows each chapter; a Conclusion, The Lamb on the Throne, and a Closing Prayer end the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
