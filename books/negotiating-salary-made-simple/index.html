Negotiating Salary Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Business & Leadership

Most people accept the first number they are offered, and that single habit, repeated across a career, costs more than almost any other financial decision they make. Negotiating Salary Made Simple is about that specific conversation — the practical mechanics of asking for more, with real language and a clear view of how the other side is thinking. A first offer, the book argues, is rarely a company's actual maximum; it is a starting point. The thirteen chapters work through the whole negotiation: how employers actually set compensation, researching your actual market value, and when to raise the topic, then the language that works, navigating the back-and-forth, and using competing offers responsibly. It also covers negotiating beyond base salary, specific difficult scenarios, what to do when it does not go the way you hoped, negotiating a raise in your current role, and negotiating early in your career, before closing on common mistakes and building negotiation as a lifelong skill. Its single piece of takeaway preparation is concrete: research your genuine market value using at least two independent sources, and prepare one specific, complete sentence stating what you are asking for and why. The book does not promise the conversation will stop being uncomfortable — it provides the specific, practical knowledge that makes moving through that discomfort worthwhile. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
