Math Made Simple

Mental Math, Everyday Shortcuts, and the Tricks Nobody Taught You in School

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Education

Six people, one dinner check, and six phones come out — because somewhere along the way, ordinary adults were taught that arithmetic belongs to machines. It does not. The methods that let a person add, multiply, divide, and estimate quickly and accurately in their head were simply never handed over: school taught the paper algorithms, and paper methods fail in the head. Math Made Simple hands over the other methods — the ones shopkeepers, carpenters, market traders, and mental calculators have always used — organized as a practical handbook you can learn from, practice with, and keep beside you: number decomposition and compensation, doubling and halving, break-apart multiplication, the Vedic shortcuts (Nikhilam near a base, squares ending in 5, vertically-and-crosswise), the Trachtenberg speed rules, division by factors and chunks, percentage anchors, fraction-decimal fluency, honest estimation, rapid squares and square roots, divisibility rules, and the everyday financial calculations of tips, taxes, stacked discounts, unit prices, interest, and wages.

Every technique is taught the same disciplined way: the easiest, most efficient method first, a simple explanation, step-by-step worked examples, why it works, when it applies and when it does not, real-world applications, practice exercises with answers, and progressively harder examples — alternates appear only where they genuinely serve a different case, and the book says why. Around the thirteen technique chapters stands the full training apparatus: a quick-reference cheat sheet, daily five-minute drills, a mastery test for every chapter, a comprehensive answer key that leaves nothing unanswered, a starting self-check so you can measure your own before-and-after, and a complete 30-day mental math training program that builds accuracy first, speed second, and confidence throughout.

The reader this book was written for is the adult who reaches for a calculator and wishes they did not have to: students who want number fluency that survives the exam hall, homeschool families who want methods worth teaching, and anyone who has ever suspected they were "not a math person" and was wrong. There is no such person — there are only people who were taught the paper methods and blamed themselves when those methods failed in the head. Learn the head methods, thirty days of five minutes at a time, and the next number life asks for — at a table, in an aisle, across a counter — is simply yours: quickly, accurately, and checked two ways.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
