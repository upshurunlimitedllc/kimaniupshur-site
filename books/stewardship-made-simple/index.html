Stewardship

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Money & Finance

Stewardship opens with a question underneath everything: whose is it, really? Its answer is the foundation of the whole book: you own nothing, you manage everything. Kimani Upshur, M.Ed., presents the reader not as an owner but as a steward — someone entrusted with God's money, time, talents, body, family, and influence for a season, who will one day give an account. Two anchors return again and again: "Now it is required that those who have been given a trust must prove faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:2) and the master who "entrusted his wealth to them" (Matthew 25:14). Fourteen chapters build from what stewardship actually is and the claim that everything belongs to God — "The earth is the LORD's, and everything in it" (Psalm 24:1) — through the parable of the talents and being faithful with little. The book then works room by room through the stewardship of money, time, gifts, the body, family, and creation, before turning to generosity as the steward's joy and the tragedy of the buried talent. It closes with accountability day, a call to live as a faithful steward every day, an appendix of straight answers on stewardship, and a closing prayer — trusted, faithful, fruitful in ordinary, daily practice. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
