Your Attention Is Being Sold

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Personal GrowthCommercial Psychology

Dana sat down at 8:40 to read for twenty minutes and call her sister, opened her phone to check one thing — and looked up at 10:25 with the book unopened, the call unmade, and the distinct feeling of having been billed. She had been. Her evening was not empty; it was spent, carefully and profitably, by an industry built to take the hours of your attention and sell them to someone else. Your Attention Is Being Sold works the reader's side of that counter. It begins with the asset itself: attention as a fixed budget of waking hours that cannot be earned back once spent — three ordinary hours a day inside ad-supported apps adds up to roughly forty-five days a year — and shows why data is only the targeting system while your minutes are what is actually sold.

Then it follows the money and opens the machinery. What an hour of you is worth to the platforms that retail it by the hour for pocket change — and recover the cost from your wallet again at the register. The feed as an auction finished before the screen lights, ranking your world by predicted engagement rather than by truth or satisfaction. The hooks: infinite scroll, autoplay, streaks, and the engineered removal of every natural stopping cue, until continuing costs nothing and stopping costs an act of will. The interruption tax — thirty notifications a day quietly consuming the equivalent of twelve working weeks a year in refocusing. The outrage loop, in which moral anger is selected, seasoned, and sold back to you daily. The comparison shelf, where your full backstage life is measured against a composite of hundreds of other people's highlights. And the deepest cost of all: fragmentation, which thins your work, your reading, your boredom, and finally your memory of your own years.

The second half is the reclamation, and it is deliberately ordinary. Audit your attention for one week with a simple ledger — chosen versus continued — and get your first honest statement of where the hours go. Change the defaults in an afternoon so the machinery has to win each minute fairly instead of collecting it from a rigged arrangement. Defend one deep hour a day, and watch it compound into hundreds of hours a year of your best mind. Then spend the asset the way its owner should: on the people, work, learning, and rest it was always meant for. No quitting the modern world, no new personality — the same levers the industry used, pulled in the other direction. Your attention is being sold. It does not have to be.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
