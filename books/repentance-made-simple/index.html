Repentance

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Faith & Doctrine

Repentance is a biblical guide to turning back to God — summed up in its own three words: Turn. Return. Renew. It begins by recovering what repentance actually is, refusing to reduce it to feeling sorry for a while: you sin, you feel guilty, you promise to do better, and by Thursday you are doing it again. The book sets repentance beside remorse, then walks through the Bible's own turning points — "Repent, then, and turn to God," confessing and forsaking sin, the prodigal son coming home in Luke 15, David's repentance in Psalm 51, and the promise of 1 John 1:9, "If we confess our sins." It follows repentance into restoration, into naming specific sins honestly, and into relationships — making things right with the people we have wronged. It then treats repentance as a daily lifestyle, considers repentance and baptism, and shows how to help others turn back gently. Each of the fourteen chapters ends with one action step — not five, not a checklist, one thing you can actually do today — and the book closes with a written closing prayer. Scripture quotations are taken from the New International Version, with some passages paraphrased for clarity and marked as such. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
