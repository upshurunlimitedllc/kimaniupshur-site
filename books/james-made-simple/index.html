James

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

James, subtitled A Biblical Guide to Faith That Works, takes up the most practical book in the New Testament: the five-chapter letter of James, the half-brother of Jesus who became a pillar of the church in Jerusalem. Its argument is the letter's own — "Show me your faith without deeds, and I will show you my faith by my deeds" (James 2:18) — and it holds James and Paul together plainly: Paul describes the root of salvation by faith, James describes its fruit in deeds. Fourteen short chapters, one for each major teaching in James, each open with Scripture and end with one action step. They cover counting trials as joy, asking in faith, being doers of the Word, refusing favoritism, faith without works, taming the tongue, wisdom from above, drawing near to God, patience in suffering, and the prayer of faith. Scripture quotations are from the New International Version (NIV), as the book's copyright page states. An appendix, Fourteen Honest Questions About Faith That Works, answers fourteen questions readers ask — beginning with whether James contradicts Paul — and the book closes with a Closing Prayer. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
