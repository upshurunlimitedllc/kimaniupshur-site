Zechariah

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Zechariah begins with a sentence that has carried many weary workers: "Not by might nor by power, but by My Spirit, says the LORD of hosts" (Zechariah 4:6). Zechariah ministered in 520 B.C. alongside Haggai to the returned exiles rebuilding Jerusalem, and his name means "the LORD remembers" — the heart of a book about God remembering His covenant, His people, and His promises. The introduction declares the book Scripture-first, with every chapter anchored in the book of Zechariah — all fourteen chapters — and its fulfillments in Jesus Christ. Thirteen chapters follow the visions and oracles in order: the call "Return to Me," horsemen among the myrtle trees, horns, craftsmen, and a city without walls, Joshua in filthy garments, the golden lampstand, the flying scroll and four chariots, the crowned Branch uniting priest and king, justice rather than fasting, and the restoration promise. The closing chapters present the King riding on a donkey, the true Shepherd and the worthless one in Zechariah 10–11, the Pierced One and the struck Shepherd, and the final triumph when the LORD shall be King. Reflection Questions, a conclusion titled "Visions. Promise. King.," and a Closing Prayer complete Zechariah. Where the returned exiles saw only rubble and opposition, Zechariah lifted their eyes to God's visions of hope and to the coming King whose work would be finished by the Spirit. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
