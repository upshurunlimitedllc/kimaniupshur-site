Error Detection Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Tech & AI

Most errors aren't caught by being smarter. They're caught by knowing where to look. Error Detection Made Simple treats catching errors as a distinct, learnable skill — separate from intelligence, separate from carefulness, and available to anyone willing to practice it. Its Introduction begins with a familiar embarrassment: a senior engineer reviews a document for the third time, signs off, and a newcomer spots a mistake sitting in plain sight on the first page, because familiarity steadily degrades a person's ability to notice errors in work they know well. The chapters build the discipline in order: what error detection actually is; where errors actually hide; how an error differs from an anomaly, which is an invitation to look closer and never a verdict on its own; building checks into your own work; catching errors in other people's work; error detection in data and numbers, through reconciliation and sanity checks; error detection in organizations; why errors survive review; the psychology of missing the obvious; building an error-detection habit; and knowing when to stop checking. The Conclusion sums the method up as the discipline of catching what almost got through: engineering distance into self-review, structuring the review of others' work around explicit categories rather than a casual read-through, and investigating before judging in either direction. Find It. Trace It. Fix It. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
