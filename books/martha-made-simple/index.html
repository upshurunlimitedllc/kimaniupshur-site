Martha

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Martha — Serving. Learning. Beloved. — follows the woman of Bethany through the three scenes the Gospels give her, tracing an arc from striving to rest. In the first, she receives Jesus into her house and is cumbered about much serving; in the second, she meets Him on the road in grief; in the third, she serves again at a dinner in Bethany, this time without complaint. Twelve chapters work through that journey: her welcome, her distraction, Jesus' gentle correction — “Martha, Martha” — Mary's choice of that good part, and the one thing needful (Luke 10:42). At the tomb, Martha makes her great confession, “I believe that thou art the Christ” (John 11), before the book turns to serving again and to three chapters on Martha and the Father, Martha and the Son, and Martha and the Holy Spirit — power, not willpower. A final chapter, “The Martha in All of Us,” gathers the story into a rhythm of rest: sit, then serve; receive, then give. A Conclusion titled “Beloved” and a Closing Prayer complete the book. The point throughout is not to stop serving, but to reorder it — beloved before useful. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
