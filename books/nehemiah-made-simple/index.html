Nehemiah

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Nehemiah was the king's cupbearer in Susa — safe, well fed, and far from home — until his brother brought word that Jerusalem's wall was broken down and its gates burned. He sat down and wept, mourned for days, fasted, and prayed. Nehemiah tells his story straight from the book of Nehemiah: the burden that started everything, the night survey of the damage, and a wall rebuilt in fifty-two days by a people who had lived with the rubble for more than a century. The ten chapters follow the work in order: the cupbearer's tears, every family building the section nearest its home, the builders holding a trowel in one hand and a sword in the other against mockery and threat, Nehemiah confronting injustice inside the walls, and his refusal of every distraction — 'I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down.' The book then turns from masonry to worship: the Law read aloud and the joy of the Lord as strength, the long confession of Nehemiah 9, and Nehemiah's final plea, 'Remember me, O my God, for good.' It is designed to be read slowly, one chapter a day. Each chapter follows the pattern its How to Use section describes: Scripture first, then teaching rooted in the text, then Reflection questions, Application steps, and a Prayer to the Father, through Jesus Christ, by the Holy Spirit. A Conclusion and a Closing Prayer complete the volume, and its closing point is plain: God still uses ordinary people with broken hearts to rebuild broken things. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
