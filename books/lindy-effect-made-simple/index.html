Lindy Effect Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Lindy Effect Made Simple: Why Old Ideas Last, How to Judge What Will Endure, and Where to Place Your Bets explains a simple pattern: the longer something has survived, the longer it is likely to keep surviving — not always, but often enough that ignoring the pattern is its own kind of mistake. The idea takes its name from a New York deli where entertainers speculated about which comedians would still be working in a decade, and concluded that a comedian who had already lasted twenty years was a safer bet for the next twenty than one who had lasted two. The book is careful about scope: the effect applies to things that do not naturally decay with age, so a book still read after a century is a better bet to be read in another century than one published last month. Thirteen chapters state what the Lindy effect actually says and why survival time predicts future survival, distinguish Lindy things from fragile things, and apply the principle to books, ideas, and culture, to technology and tools, and to habits and institutions. It then names the traps: confusing old with good, the cases where the effect does not apply, and the common mistakes that misuse the principle. Closing chapters cover evaluating new trends, personal decision making, balancing Lindy wisdom with genuine innovation, and building an ongoing habit of judging what lasts — so readers can place their attention and trust more wisely. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
