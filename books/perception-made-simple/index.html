Perception Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Perception Made Simple — How What You See Shapes What You Believe and the Decisions You Make — starts from an everyday puzzle: two people can look at the same situation and see completely different things. The same conversation is remembered differently by the two people who had it; the same policy is experienced as oppressive by one person and reasonable by another. The book explains why: perception is not passive recording — the eye is not a camera — but an active process that constructs meaning from experience, and what you see determines what you believe and how you act. Ten short chapters build the account in order: what perception is, seeing versus interpreting, why people see things differently, and the roles of experience and of attention and focus; then perception's connection to belief and to decision making, and how perception becomes distorted. The final chapters turn practical — improving perception and seeing more clearly — before a conclusion, Perception and Reality, draws the threads together. It serves any reader whose judgments, decisions, or disagreements keep turning on how things look from where they stand. The gain is a clearer account of the step between seeing and believing — and tools for checking that step before acting on it. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
