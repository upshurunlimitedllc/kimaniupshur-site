Skepticism

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Skepticism begins with a question you were afraid to ask out loud. Nobody drifts into doubt on purpose, this book argues — it starts with an honest question, a disappointment, an unanswered prayer — and doubt itself is not the enemy. Its promise is Jesus' own: "Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and the door will be opened to you" (Matthew 7:7–8). God, it says, has an open-door policy for honest seekers; you do not need to clean yourself up or believe first in order to ask. Scripture quotations are from the New International Version. Fourteen chapters walk the road from doubt to faith — asked, answered, assured. The reader meets Thomas and Scripture's other honest doubters, names the roots of doubt, and learns the Berean way: receiving the message with eagerness while examining "the Scriptures every day to see if what Paul said was true" (Acts 17:11). The book weighs faith and reason as friends, tests whether we can trust the Bible, and faces the question of who Jesus really is, Thomas's confession — "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28) — included. It wrestles the hard questions, God's silence, and unanswered prayer, believes with the desperate father's "help me overcome my unbelief" (Mark 9:24), and ends where doubters become witnesses. An appendix of straight answers and a closing prayer complete the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
