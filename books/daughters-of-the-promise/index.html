Daughters of the Promise

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

From Eve to Phoebe, the promise of God has always traveled through daughters. Daughters of the Promise is a twelve-chapter walk through the women of Scripture, following one thread through all of them: the promise first spoken in Genesis 3:15, and the daughters who carried it — not around them, but through them. The chapters move in order: Eve; Sarah and Hagar; Rebekah, Rachel, and Leah; Tamar; Miriam and Deborah; Ruth; Hannah and Abigail; Esther; Mary the mother of Jesus; Mary Magdalene and the women of the resurrection morning; Lydia, Priscilla, and Phoebe; and the daughters of the promise today. Each chapter opens with a Scripture epigraph, walks through the passage in plain language, and closes with reflection and application. The book names hard things plainly — slavery, barrenness, betrayal, violence — because the text names them; it never invents what Scripture does not say, and it marks the difference between what the text states and what interpreters conclude. Three words give every chapter its rhythm: Seen — God sees the women the world overlooks; Chosen — He chooses them to carry the promise; Sent — He sends them, as He sent Mary Magdalene to the apostles with the first resurrection sermon. Written for women and for men, because the daughters were never the whole story, but the story is not whole without them. Scripture references are paraphrased from the English Standard Version (ESV). Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
