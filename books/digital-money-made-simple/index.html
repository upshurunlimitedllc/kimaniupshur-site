Digital Money Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Money is being redesigned in public, and almost nobody understands the proposals. Digital Money Made Simple fixes that, starting with what money is — trust plus a ledger — and its three jobs: medium of exchange, unit of account, and store of value, with hyperinflation's warnings from Weimar Germany, Zimbabwe, and Venezuela. It describes cash as it actually is — peer-to-peer, instant, anonymous, and permissionless, working without electricity or an account, with Sweden as the closest cashless case — then traces digital payments through cards, Kenya's M-Pesa, and phone wallets, including the hidden interchange fees of around two to three percent. A CBDC is defined precisely: a digital form of official currency issued directly by the central bank — retail versus wholesale, and distinct from bank deposits in who issues it and whose ledger it sits on, in cash-like and account-like designs. The documented programs follow: the Bahamas' Sand Dollar (launched October 2020, the first fully deployed CBDC), China's e-CNY pilots, Nigeria's eNaira, Jamaica's JAM-DEX (2022), India's e-rupee pilots, and the European Central Bank's digital euro under development — with no U.S. retail CBDC launched or committed. Stablecoins (USDT and USDC) are explained as private dollar-pegged tokens dependent on issuer reserves, contrasted with both crypto speculation and government money, with the 2022 TerraUSD collapse as the cautionary case. Programmable money gets three evaluation questions — who programs, what limits, and can you opt out — including the expiring-money thought experiment. Then you get the honest case for and against these developments, the Bible's teaching on money, and practical steps to prepare. Twelve chapters. Scripture quotations are from the ESV. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
