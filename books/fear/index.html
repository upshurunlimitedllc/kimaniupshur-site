Fear

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Faith & Doctrine

Fear is not the problem. What you do with it is. Fear opens with that distinction and treats fear as a system: it has a trigger, a reaction, and a loop that reinforces itself every time you avoid what frightens you. Most people try to eliminate fear, and that approach fails because fear cannot be removed — it is built in, the brain's threat detection and response system, in which the amygdala triggers adrenaline and cortisol in milliseconds, before slower evaluation can judge whether a threat is real. The eight chapters work through that system in order: the function of fear; how fear distorts thinking, through overestimation and narrowing; fear and decision making; the types of fear; the fear loop itself; breaking the loop; using fear correctly; and building control over time. Breaking the loop is practical work, not motivation: gradual exposure that starts with versions of the feared situation producing manageable discomfort, corrective experiences of encountering what is feared and surviving it, and system building — scheduled exposures, environments that make avoidance less convenient, and commitments to other people that create accountability. Mastery, as the book defines it, does not mean the absence of fear; it means fear no longer has veto power over your decisions, so fear becomes information you can use rather than a command you must obey. This book is for educational purposes only and does not constitute psychological, therapeutic, or medical advice. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
