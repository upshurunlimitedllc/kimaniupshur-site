Digital Minimalism Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Tech & AI

You didn't choose most of your phone habits. A team of engineers optimized them for you — infinite scroll, autoplay, and variable-reward notifications designed and tested to keep you looking longer than you would ever choose on your own. Digital Minimalism Made Simple is about taking that choice back: not rejecting technology, but deciding deliberately what role it actually plays in your life. The book begins with an honest audit of your actual usage, then walks through a thirty-day digital declutter and how to reintroduce technology deliberately, keeping only what serves something you genuinely value. Notifications come next — the single biggest lever — followed by social media without fully quitting, and the difference your phone's physical presence makes simply by being in the room. Later chapters ask what actually fills the time minimalism frees, why solitude matters and constant connectivity crowds it out, and how digital minimalism works for families and for work, where the always-on expectation never quite lets go. The book is honest about the failure mode too: minimalism itself can become its own obsession, one more system to perfect instead of a life to live. The closing chapters focus on sustaining the practice long-term, through ongoing evaluation rather than a one-time purge. Start with your notification settings, the book advises — turn off what doesn't need your real-time attention, and notice how much of your day was being shaped by interruptions you never chose. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
