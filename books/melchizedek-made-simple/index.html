Melchizedek

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Melchizedek — The Priest Forever Who Prefigures Christ — takes up a figure who appears in only three sentences of Genesis, yet on whom the New Testament builds a great doctrine. Melchizedek was king of Salem and priest of the most high God, who met Abraham after the slaughter of the kings with bread and wine and a blessing, and received his tithe (Genesis 14:18-20). He has no recorded genealogy, birth, or death. Twelve chapters follow him through Scripture: the priest of the Most High God, bread and wine after the battle, the tithe that testifies, the silence of Scripture, a new order, the oath God swore (Psalm 110:4), a better covenant's guarantee, Christ ever living to intercede, and the call to draw near with boldness. The book's "How to Use This Book" sets its pattern: Scripture first, then teaching, reflection questions, application steps, and a prayer. Melchizedek presents this king-priest as a type of Jesus Christ — King of Righteousness, King of Peace, and priest forever (Hebrews 7) — and closes with a conclusion and a Closing Prayer. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
