Isaiah

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Isaiah, subtitled A Biblical Guide to the Prince of Prophets, walks through the longest prophetic book in the Bible through the moment that made the prophet: the temple vision of Isaiah 6, the coal that cleansed his lips, and his answer, "Here am I. Send me." It presents Isaiah's sixty-year ministry under kings Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah as cleansed, called, and comforted — and reads the prophet throughout as pointing to Jesus Christ, in His virgin birth (Isaiah 7:14), His titles (9:6), His suffering (53), and His kingdom. Fourteen chapters each open with Scripture and end with a single action step. They move from The Vision That Changes Everything and Cleansed by Fire through Comfort, Comfort My People, God With Us, and The Suffering Servant to Wings Like Eagles, Do Not Fear (Isaiah 41:10), The Fast God Chooses, and New Heavens, New Earth (65:17). Scripture quotations are from the New International Version (NIV), as the book's copyright page states. An appendix, Twelve Honest Questions About Isaiah, answers twelve questions readers ask about the prophet, and the book closes with a Closing Prayer. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
