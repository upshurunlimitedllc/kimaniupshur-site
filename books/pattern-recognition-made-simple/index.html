Pattern Recognition Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Tech & AI

Your brain is always looking for patterns. It finds them in data, in people, in markets, and in situations, even when no pattern exists. Pattern Recognition Made Simple teaches the harder skill: knowing which patterns are real, which ones are noise, and what to do with the difference. It opens with the pattern-seeking brain and a famous 1944 experiment in which viewers turned moving triangles and a circle into a full drama of motives and personalities the shapes never had. Thirteen chapters then build the discipline step by step: what pattern recognition actually is, how the brain finds structure, and how expertise sharpens the expert's eye. The book separates real patterns from coincidence, names apophenia, the habit of seeing patterns that are not there, and applies pattern recognition to decision-making, emotional patterns and behavioral loops, and reading people and groups. It turns to patterns in business and markets, to pattern blindness and what you systematically miss, and to pattern recognition in artificial intelligence. It closes by showing how to improve your pattern recognition and build a pattern-aware mind, so the structure you see in a complex world is the structure that is actually there. It also stresses that none of these improvements happen automatically or quickly, but that they are genuine and cumulative for anyone willing to practise deliberately. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
