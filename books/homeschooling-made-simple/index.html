Homeschooling Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Family & Relationships

Homeschooling teaches what its opening note calls the real machinery of homeschooling — not the highlight reel, not the horror stories, but the actual mechanics of how ordinary parents teach their children at home and do it well. Written by an educator who states plainly, “I'm also a Christian, and this book is written from a Christian worldview,” it assumes the reader is starting from zero and aims to leave them with a complete first-year plan. Thirteen chapters begin with the honest decision and the law, then set out the five ways to do this, the daily rhythm, teaching three ages at once in a one-room-schoolhouse approach, and the socialization question answered honestly. They continue through curriculum shopping without drowning, learning difficulties and special needs, high school at home with transcripts, credits, and college, the money side and what homeschooling really costs, the hard seasons of burnout and doubt, measuring success, and the long game of raising adults, not just students. A conclusion titled “The Kitchen Table” and an appendix, “The First-Year Launch Checklist,” close the book. The framework it sets out is consistent throughout: every chapter ends with one action step, and doing those steps in order forms the book's spine. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
