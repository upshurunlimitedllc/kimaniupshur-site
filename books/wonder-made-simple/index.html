Wonder Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Wonder is about never losing your wonder at God — amazed, awed, alive. Its introduction opens with Psalm 19:1, “The heavens declare the glory of God,” and asks when you were last truly amazed: not entertained or briefly impressed, but stopped in your tracks, breath caught, heart wide open. Wonder, it argues, is not a nice extra for sentimental people; it is the right response of the human heart to the God who is really there, and most readers have lost it slowly, trading awe for analysis and reverence for routine. Across fourteen chapters, the book relearns wonder from Scripture itself. David looking up in Psalm 8, Isaiah's “Holy, holy, holy” in Isaiah 6, Moses taking off his sandals in Exodus 3, and the fear of the Lord in Proverbs 9:10 come first. Then come becoming like little children, the crowds who never saw anything like Jesus' miracles, the disciples asking “Who is this?” in the storm, the empty tomb, and Paul's outburst in Romans 11:33 at the depth of God's riches. Later chapters ask when wonder dies, how to recover it by remembering God's deeds, and how to keep it in the dark, with Job. An appendix of ten honest questions, a throne-room conclusion, and a closing prayer complete the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
