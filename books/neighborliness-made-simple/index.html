Neighborliness Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

You know their names — the couple next door, the elderly woman across the street, the man who never waves back. They are the people closest to your daily life and, if you are honest, the people your faith touches least. Neighborliness takes up Jesus' answer to the lawyer's question, 'Who is my neighbor?': the Good Samaritan of Luke 10, and the second greatest commandment, 'Love your neighbor as yourself.' Its motto is three words: Near. Kind. Present. Across twelve chapters the book works out what that love looks like at your own address: hospitality, bearing burdens, the ministry of presence, peacemaking, generosity next door, praying for neighbors, and forgiving neighbors — quickly, completely, and repeatedly, as Christ forgave you. It draws on the early church of Acts 2, whose shared homes and tables turned a neighborhood upside down, and closes by treating your street as a mission field. An appendix, Straight Answers on Neighborliness, takes up difficult neighbors, boundaries, safety, and neighbors who believe differently. Scripture quotations are, as the book itself notes, taken from the New International Version unless otherwise noted. The book ends where it began: the lawyer asked who his neighbor was, and Jesus answered, 'Go and do likewise.' A Closing Prayer sends the reader out as a neighbor-missionary, starting at the front door. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
