Church History Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Most Christians can name the twelve disciples and know Luther nailed something to a door — and after that, church history is a blur. Church History Made Simple tells the full two-thousand-year story plainly, in twelve short chapters read one a day at about ten minutes each: every chapter opens with a Scripture passage, tells the plain story of what happened, and closes with a 'Your move this week' section. The journey runs in three movements — the founding, the growing, and the going — anchored on Jesus' promise in Matthew 16:18: 'I will build my church.' You will meet the apostles and Pentecost, the persecuted church and its martyrs — Polycarp, Perpetua, and Justin Martyr — and Constantine and the Edict of Milan in 313. The councils follow — Nicaea (325), Constantinople (381), Ephesus (431), and Chalcedon (451) — with the creeds presented as guardrails tested against Scripture. Then come bishops and basilicas, the recognition of the twenty-seven-book New Testament, Jerome's Latin Vulgate, Augustine of Hippo, and monks including Anthony, Benedict, and Patrick. The Middle Ages are held with their glory and shame together — universities, hospitals, cathedrals, and Aquinas alongside the East–West split of 1054, the Crusades, indulgences, Wycliffe, and Hus — before Luther, Romans 1:17, October 31, 1517, and the five solas: Sola Scriptura, Sola fide, Sola gratia, Solus Christus, Soli Deo gloria. You don't need a background in theology or history — if you follow Jesus, the church's story is your story. Scripture quotations are from the ESV. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
