Information Quality Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Information Quality Made Simple by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed. — 'How to Filter Bad Information, Detect Signal, and Think Clearly in a Noisy World' — opens with its verdict: most people are overinformed and undercalibrated; they consume enormous amounts of information, and almost none of it improves their thinking. Its introduction, 'The Information Problem', names the paradox — more information, not less confusion; more access, not better judgment — and defines its subject: high-quality information is accurate, reliable, calibrated (the certainty it communicates matches the strength of the evidence), and relevant. Thirteen chapters diagnose the environment before repairing the reader's place in it: why bad information spreads, signal versus noise, information overload, media distortion, the attention economy, confirmation bias and the closed information loop, narrative and manipulation, credibility and source quality, propaganda and persuasion, social media as cognitive pollution, and truth versus persuasion. The final chapters turn constructive — Chapter 12, 'Building Better Information Filters', begins with a source audit of the reader's own intake, and Chapter 13 takes up thinking clearly in a noisy world. The conclusion, 'Information That Actually Helps', is candid that the structural problem will not fix itself, and commends what is available now: the individual practice of information quality management — improving the signal-to-noise ratio of your personal information environment and building the evaluation skills to extract genuine signal from what arrives. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
