Thomas

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Bible People & Stories

Thomas has been called “Doubting Thomas” for two thousand years, and Thomas argues the nickname is unfair. He was the honest doubter: the disciple who named his doubts out loud, was answered not with a rebuke but with the wounds of the risen Christ, and ended with the highest confession in the Gospels, “My Lord and my God” (John 20:28). The book’s How to Use page sets a steady pattern for its twelve chapters — Scripture first, then teaching rooted in the text, Reflection questions, Application steps, and a Prayer to the Father, through Jesus Christ, by the Holy Spirit. The chapters follow Thomas through the Gospels: the loyal follower who said, “Let us also go, that we may die with him” (John 11:16), the questioner whose words drew out “I am the way, the truth, and the life” (John 14:5), the night he missed, his terms — “Except I shall see” — and the meeting eight days later when Jesus offered His hands and His side (John 20:24–29). Later chapters distinguish honest doubt from unbelief, show how to bring your questions to Christ rather than to the mockers, and follow Thomas, in church tradition, as the apostle who carried the gospel to India. A Closing Prayer gathers the confession. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
