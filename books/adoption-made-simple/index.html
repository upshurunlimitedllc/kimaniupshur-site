Adoption

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Family & Relationships

Adoption opens with two people lying awake: a child in a bed that isn't theirs, wondering if anyone is coming, and a would-be parent with papers on the table and a home study on the calendar. Its introduction, "Chosen on Purpose," states the book's central conviction: "Adoption is not Plan B. It is God's idea." The gospel itself, Adoption argues, is an adoption story — God the Father chose us, Jesus Christ made the way by His death and resurrection, and the Holy Spirit brought us home — and every earthly adoption done in love echoes that greater one. The book is written tenderly for the whole adoption triad: adoptees, birth parents, adoptive parents, families still waiting, and the church. Fourteen chapters move from theology to practice: what "chosen" means in the Bible's words, God setting the lonely in families, the three roads of foster, private, and international adoption, counting the cost and bringing it to God, the home study without the performance, and the long wait. Later chapters honor the birth family, face the loss every adoption begins with, and take up attachment, telling the child's story, the hard questions kids ask, becoming one family across many colors, persevering when it gets hard, and forever family as covenant love practiced on ordinary days. Each chapter ends with one action step. An appendix, "Straight Answers on Adoption," a conclusion, "The Father Is Still Adopting," and a closing prayer complete the book. Scripture quotations are taken from the New International Version (NIV). Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
