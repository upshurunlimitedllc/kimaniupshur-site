Information Processing Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Information Processing Made Simple by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed. — 'How the Mind Filters, Organizes, and Uses Information to Make Decisions' — starts from a simple observation: two people can receive the same information, read the same report or hear the same conversation, and reach completely different conclusions. What matters is not what you receive; it is how you process it. In ten chapters and a conclusion, the book lays out processing as an active, multi-stage construction rather than passive reception. Early chapters distinguish input from processing and take up filtering and organizing — the attentional filter and the schemas that sort what gets in — and Chapter 1 sets out the book's two operating levels, automatic processing (fast, parallel, effortless) and deliberate processing (slow, sequential, effortful, conscious). Middle chapters cover interpretation and meaning, the mental models that shape processing, and the systematic processing errors those same mechanisms produce. The closing chapters weigh speed against accuracy and turn to improvement: the conclusion, 'Processing Is Everything', names the practices the book commends — deliberate goal specification, periodic widening, explicit structuring, alternative interpretation, and the deliberate pause — as the route to better judgment and decision-making. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
