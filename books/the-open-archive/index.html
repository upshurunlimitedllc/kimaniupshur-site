The Open Archive

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Fiction

Day 986. Delphine's two sentences release everything the cold room holds. At 0600 the next morning, archivist Miriam opens the Table's archive herself — the chains, the findings, the trust's accounting, the forty-seven names — and the requests begin: eleven by midday, forty by evening, a thousand downloads in four days. The Open Archive follows what happens when a sealed record becomes public: families hear it first, in a room, from a person, with water on the table; nothing is redacted and no one is refused. Mara Kade reads her father's name among the forty-seven and files the first claim against the nine-hundred-million-credit trust. Director Pryce circulates a proposal to close the archive again. The archive does not argue. It outlasts. Book Four of the Halcyon Protocol series. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
