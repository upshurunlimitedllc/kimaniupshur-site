Onboarding Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Onboarding Made Simple is about the structured process that turns a good hiring decision into a new hire who is genuinely productive — instead of one left to figure everything out alone. Its starting observation is blunt: a great hire can still fail in the first ninety days, not because the wrong person was picked, but because nobody actually set them up to succeed. A laptop, a login, and a brief tour are not onboarding, and the slow drift that follows is a process failure, not a hiring mistake. The book builds the alternative across thirteen chapters. Onboarding starts before day one, with deliberate preparation; the first week is designed rather than improvised; and a ninety-day arc gives the new hire a real trajectory. It separates the manager's role from the onboarding buddy's, builds genuine connection alongside technical competence, and sets expectations clearly enough to be measured. Further chapters cover remote and hybrid employees, feedback during the early period, the honest case where onboarding reveals a genuine mismatch, onboarding into leadership roles, and measuring whether the process actually works — ending with building a system an organisation will actually reuse. It serves managers, team leads, and anyone responsible for a new hire's first months. Its one practice, if a reader takes nothing else, is concrete: define specific milestones for the first thirty, sixty, and ninety days, in writing, before the new hire even starts. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
