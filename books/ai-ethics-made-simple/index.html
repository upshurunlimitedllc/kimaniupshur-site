AI Ethics

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Tech & AI

Machines don't have morals. You do. That is the sentence AI Ethics hangs on, and its answer to every new technology question is an old one: Scripture is the final authority on right and wrong. Subtitled "A Biblical Guide to Right and Wrong in the Age of AI," the book works through the moral questions artificial intelligence raises in fourteen chapters, with every Scripture quotation drawn from the King James Version. It begins where the Bible begins — human beings made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27) — which is why no machine can carry moral weight and no user can hide behind "the computer did it." From there it takes up truth in the age of deepfakes, fairness and bias under the God who hates rigged scales, work and the dignity of labor, and privacy, before its heaviest chapter: AI in war and surveillance, where life-and-death decisions must remain with accountable human agents (Romans 13:4). Later chapters cover raising kids in the age of AI, keeping your integrity when cheating is easy, loving your neighbor in a digital world, the temptation of tech worship, and what AI cannot do — it has no soul, and it cannot repent, pray, love, worship, or save. An appendix of straight answers, a closing prayer, and a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ, with the Sinner's Prayer, close the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
