Widow

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Widow is a biblical guide to grieving, healing, and beginning again. A gentle note at the front is clear about its limits: this is biblical encouragement, not professional financial or legal advice, and a trusted Christian counselor or support group can help when grief feels too heavy to carry. Its introduction, “Rebuilding Life When Half Your Heart Is in Heaven,” begins where the reader is — with the day everything changed, and with grief described as love with nowhere to go. Across fourteen chapters, the book grounds comfort in who God is: the defender of widows, anchored in Psalm 68:5, and the Jesus who stopped for the widow of Nain because He saw her. It then turns practical — first things first, the loneliness, raising kids on your own, and letting the church carry you, as James 1:27 envisions. Later chapters name grief coming in waves, ask “Who am I now?,” treat dating again, and show how to honor a husband's memory without living in the past. The closing chapters point toward a new purpose and the reunion hope, held against 1 Thessalonians 4:13 and the promise that God will wipe away every tear. An appendix of questions and answers, a conclusion of grace for the road ahead, and a closing prayer complete the book. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
