Brain Lying Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Health & Wellness

You trust your own thinking. You should be more careful. Brain Lying Made Simple — whose manuscript title page reads Your Brain Is Lying to You: How Your Own Mind Distorts Reality, and How to Think Anyway — argues that the brain is not a truth machine: it is optimized for survival, not accuracy, and its shortcuts now misfire in modern decisions. Eleven chapters name the distortions one by one. Confirmation bias makes you see only what you are looking for; the narrative fallacy makes you invent stories; the availability heuristic makes whatever comes to mind first feel most true. Loss aversion makes losing hurt about twice as much as winning feels good, the Dunning-Kruger effect makes the least informed the most confident, and sunk-cost thinking, emotional reasoning, and the planning fallacy keep you holding on, feeling your way to facts, and underestimating every timeline. Each chapter closes with Key Takeaways that compress the pattern into points you can remember. The final chapters turn corrective: thinking better despite a brain that lies, using decision journals, pre-mortems, stop rules, and challengers, and a Practical Reset Plan that starts with thirty days of recording your significant decisions and reviewing them for your personal bias profile. A short, sharp primer on thinking clearly about your own thinking. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
