Virtue

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Christian Living

Pure. Excellent. Praiseworthy. Three words from Philippians 4:8 — and they are the whole mission of this book.

God does not call His people to be merely "good enough," merely "not too bad," merely "better than the neighbors." He calls us to virtue: moral excellence, shining clarity, a life that stands out in a compromising culture the way a frost-covered field stands out at dawn — clean, crisp, unmistakable. Virtue is a Bible-first guide to that life.

The apostle Peter gives us a ladder in 2 Peter 1:5-7: begin with faith, then "add to your faith virtue" — the Greek word is aretē, moral excellence. Virtue is the second rung, and you cannot climb without it. Many believers have faith but have skipped virtue. This book closes the gap. Fourteen Scripture-led chapters cover what virtue actually is — God's definition — honesty as the first excellence, being noble in a mocking culture, staying pure when no one sees, the Joseph standard of fleeing compromise, and the grace that says excellent, not perfect. It shows virtue as identity — a royal priesthood — and gets concrete: the virtuous woman in strength, honor, and excellence; the virtuous man in a caving world; virtue at work when the boss isn't watching; raising virtuous children with the home as the first school; and "His divine power" that gives us everything we need. Fourteen questions in the appendix help you run a virtue audit.

For anyone ready to add excellence to their faith. Shine like frost.
