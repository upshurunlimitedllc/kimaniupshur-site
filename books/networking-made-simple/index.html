Networking Made Simple

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Business & Leadership

Networking Made Simple rejects the version of networking most people dread: working a room, collecting business cards, and pretending to care about strangers until something useful falls out of the conversation. That version, Kimani Upshur argues, is exhausting, it does not work, and almost nobody who is actually good at this does it that way. The alternative is ordinary human curiosity and follow-through, applied consistently in professional contexts over years, until decency compounds into a real network. The book's subtitle states its aim: how to build real professional relationships without the fake handshakes. Thirteen chapters explain why most networking advice fails and what networking actually is, then move through auditing the network you already have, the weak-tie advantage, and starting a real conversation. Middle chapters cover giving before asking, the art of the follow-up, networking events without the cringe, building relationships online, and networking inside your own organization. The closing chapters turn to staying in touch without being weird about it, turning relationships into opportunities, and maintaining a network for life. The test throughout is simple: whether the other person experiences you as genuinely interested in them, or as extracting value from them. Relationships, not contact lists, are the system being built here, one relationship at a time. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
