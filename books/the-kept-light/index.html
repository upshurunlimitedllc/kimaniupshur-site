THE KEPT LIGHT

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

Fiction

The road is over. The keeping has begun.

The Pilgrim Fleet has arrived: seven ships at anchor above the Shore, and eighty-five and a half thousand people coming down by lot, family by family, to a world that has promised them nothing. Commodore Petra Voss opens the keeping account and reads it entire — five hundred fifty days of stores, the emergency reserve fenced, and one column she leaves deliberately blank: Expected, from the ground. Nothing entered. Hope is not a column in her book.

So the fleet must ask the ground to feed it, and asking has a price. Captain Hiram Bell's planting program would commit a third of the tall grain and a third of the beans — seed gathered on dying Vesper and kept forty years by Barzillai, the seed store's keeper, seed that cannot be replaced at any price — to fields whose seasons no one alive can read. Dr. Silas Ward prices the other side at full strength: wait a year, buy a calendar, lose nothing but patience. The council votes to plant, five to two, with the reserve fenced and the children's ration a first charge — and the year that follows gives them a school bell and a seed store, a water plant and a hospital descending in stages, a field that fails and is entered as failed, its cause not established, and a harvest weighed sack by weighed sack before a single figure is believed. It gives them the settlement's first birth, and its first grave. And on one evening, in both of the fleet's two countries at once — the ground and the sky — the whole people sits down at one hour to one table, and says the creed it carried across the dark.

On another shore of the same story, in a town called New Canaan, Pastor Eliab Hale keeps a licensed chapel for a registry that counts his hours and tallies his congregation — while the real congregation meets where it has always met, in a borrowed barn, in thirty-one chairs, two of them standing empty for the taken. Every night he lights the lamp in his front window, at full wick: a lamp no filing covers, that he has never asked permission to burn. Somewhere in the registry's shelves, a file under the standing reference the Hale lamps waits on a requisition that lacks one thing. A signature.

Captain-Pastor Jonah Hale must now lead a people who are learning the hardest navigation of all — staying. Mara Hale keeps their names, at their true size. Selah Rennick keeps the office that listens. And a thirteen-year-old apprentice in the seed store is learning what the whole fleet is learning: that a thing is kept by entering it honestly, at its true size, and refusing to call it more than it is.

The light is lit. The keeping continues.

"They hunt the light. We carry it."

The Kept Light is Book Four of The Pilgrim Fleet, following The Eaten Stars (Book One), The Lantern Road (Book Two), and The Farther Shore (Book Three). The voyage continues — the descent is unfinished, the second asking is in the ground, and the fleet's story is still being written. New readers can begin the voyage with The Eaten Stars.
