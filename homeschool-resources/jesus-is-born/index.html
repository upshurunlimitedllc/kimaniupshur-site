Good Tidings of Great Joy (Jesus Is Born)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Luke 2:1–20 (KJV)

Big Idea: God kept His promise and sent His Son — Jesus was born in Bethlehem, the Savior, Christ the Lord.

Memory Verse: "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." — Luke 2:11 (KJV)

You Will Need: A small box or basket lined with cloth (a simple "manger"), Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "The Greatest Announcement" (5 minutes)

Ask: "If the most important baby in history was born, who should hear about it first — kings in a palace, or shepherds in a field?" Let the kids guess. "God made the announcement His way — and who He told first tells us something wonderful about who Jesus came for."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

Caesar Augustus, the ruler of the Roman world, sent out a decree that all the world should be taxed, and everyone had to travel to his own city to be enrolled. So Joseph went up from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the city of David, because Joseph was of David's family — and Mary went with him, expecting the child the angel had promised.

While they were in Bethlehem, the time came for the baby to be born. "And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn" (Luke 2:7, KJV). The Son of God entered the world not in a palace but in a feeding trough, because there was no room in the inn.

That night, shepherds were in the fields nearby, keeping watch over their flocks. Suddenly the angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were very afraid. But the angel said: "Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people. For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord" (Luke 2:10–11, KJV). The sign would be a baby wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger. Then the sky filled with a multitude of the heavenly host, praising God: "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men" (Luke 2:14, KJV).

The shepherds hurried to Bethlehem and found Mary, Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger — everything just as they had been told. Then they spread the news abroad, and everyone who heard it wondered. And the shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen.

The Point: Bethlehem was not an accident. God had promised a Savior from David's line in David's city — and even a Roman emperor's decree served God's plan, bringing Joseph and Mary to exactly the right place. Jesus's birth is God keeping His word. And look at how He came: not with armies, not in a palace, but as a baby in a manger, announced first to shepherds. The Savior came lowly, and He came for all people — the angel's words say so: good tidings of great joy, "which shall be to all people."

The Application: The shepherds did three things we can do: they believed the message, they went to see Jesus, and they told others what they had heard and seen. Christmas is not only about a baby in a manger — it is about God sending His Son to be our Savior. Like the shepherds, we can come to Him, worship Him, and spread the good news.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Joseph and Mary travel to Bethlehem? How did God use even Caesar's decree to keep His promise?
  2. Why do you think Jesus was born in a manger instead of a palace? What does that show about Him?
  3. The angel called the news "good tidings of great joy... to all people." Who is the Savior for?
  4. What did the shepherds do after they saw Jesus? What can we do the same way?
  5. Mary "kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart" (Luke 2:19). What about Jesus's birth do you want to ponder — to think about deeply?

Hands-On Activity — "Manger Announcement" (10 minutes)

Each child makes a simple folded card: on the front, a manger drawn or made from paper strips with the cloth-lined box as the model; inside, they copy the angel's announcement from Luke 2:11 in their best handwriting: "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." Say: "The shepherds spread the news abroad. This card is your announcement — give it to someone this week and tell them the good news yourself."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, thank You for keeping Your promise and sending Your Son. Thank You that Jesus is our Savior, born for all people. Help us to believe, to worship, and to tell others the good tidings of great joy. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Luke 2:1–20 together, ideally in the evening. Each person shares their favorite detail of the story and one reason Jesus's birth gives them joy. Sing or play a Christmas hymn together. Memory verse for the week: "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord" (Luke 2:11, KJV).
