Jesus Came to Zacchaeus's House (Zacchaeus)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Luke 19:1–10 (KJV)

Big Idea: Jesus is the Son of Man who came to seek and to save the lost — and a life He saves is a life He changes.

Memory Verse: "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost." — Luke 19:10 (KJV)

You Will Need: A step stool or sturdy chair (for the opening activity, supervised), four paper "coins" and a small envelope per child (for the hands-on activity), Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Too Short to See" (5 minutes)

Stand the children in a group and hold a picture or object just above their eye level, behind the front row. Ask the shorter children at the back: "Can you see? What could you do?" Let one child (supervised) stand on the step stool: "Now you can see over everyone! Zacchaeus had exactly this problem — and his solution is in today's story."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

Jesus was passing through Jericho, and a man named Zacchaeus wanted to see Him. Zacchaeus was the chief tax collector, and he was rich — but tax collectors in those days were hated, because many of them took more money from people than they owed. And there was another problem: Zacchaeus was small. The crowd was thick, and he could not see over it. So Zacchaeus ran ahead and climbed up into a sycamore tree, because Jesus was going to pass that way.

When Jesus came to the place, He looked up, saw Zacchaeus, and said: "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house" (Luke 19:5, KJV). Zacchaeus hurried down and received Jesus joyfully. But the crowd murmured, because Jesus had gone to be the guest of a man they called a sinner.

Then Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord: "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold" (Luke 19:8, KJV). Meeting Jesus had changed Zacchaeus's heart — the man who had taken from people now wanted to give, and to make right what he had done wrong.

Jesus answered: "This day is salvation come to this house" (Luke 19:9, KJV). And then Jesus said why He had come at all: "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" (Luke 19:10, KJV).

The Point: Jesus did not wait for Zacchaeus to climb down, clean up, and come find Him. Jesus sought Zacchaeus — He knew his name, called him out of the tree, and came to his house. That is who Jesus is: the Son of Man who seeks and saves the lost. Zacchaeus was lost in greed and sin, and Jesus found him. Nobody is too small, too far, or too sinful for Jesus to seek.

The Application: When Jesus saves us, He changes us. Zacchaeus's giving and his fourfold restoring were not what saved him — Jesus saved him — but they were the fruit of a changed heart. When we have done wrong, following Jesus means making it right where we can: saying sorry, giving back, and living generously.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why did the crowd dislike Zacchaeus? Why did they murmur when Jesus went to his house?
  2. Jesus knew Zacchaeus's name and called him personally. What does that tell us about Jesus?
  3. What changed in Zacchaeus after he met Jesus? How could people around him tell?
  4. Jesus said He came "to seek and to save that which was lost." Who is "the lost"? Can anyone be too lost for Jesus to seek?
  5. Is there something you need to make right with someone, like Zacchaeus did? What would that look like?

Hands-On Activity — "Make It Right" (10 minutes)

Give each child four paper "coins" and a small envelope. Walk through Zacchaeus's own words (Luke 19:8): he gave half of his goods to the poor, and if he had taken anything wrongly, he restored it fourfold. Have each child write or draw on their envelope one real "make it right" action they can do this week (an apology, returning something, sharing something they value, giving to someone in need), and place one paper coin inside for each person they want to make things right with. Say: "Jesus saved Zacchaeus first — the giving came after, from a changed heart. Our envelope is the same: not paying Jesus back, but showing our hearts have changed."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, thank You that Jesus came to seek and to save the lost. Thank You for seeking us. Change our hearts like You changed Zacchaeus's, and help us make right what we have done wrong. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Luke 19:1–10 together. Then, as a family, talk about "making it right": is there an apology owed, something to return, or a kindness to repay? Choose one and do it this week. Memory verse for the week: "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" (Luke 19:10, KJV).
