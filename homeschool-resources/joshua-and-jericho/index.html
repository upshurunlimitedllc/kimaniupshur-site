The Wall Fell Down (Joshua and Jericho)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Joshua 6 (KJV)

Big Idea: God keeps His promises and gives victory to His people — we obey His way, and He does what only He can do.

Memory Verse: "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city." — Joshua 6:16 (KJV)

You Will Need: Building blocks or cups to build a "wall" for the opening activity, Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "An Unbreakable Wall?" (5 minutes)

Build a small wall of blocks on the table. Say: "This is Jericho — a city with massive walls, gates shut tight, an army inside. Israel has no battering rams, no siege engines — and God says the way to take the city is to walk around it and shout. Does that sound like a battle plan?" Let the kids react. "Let's see what happened when God's people did it God's way."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

After Moses died, God raised up Joshua to lead Israel into the promised land. The first city in their path was Jericho — shut up tight, because its people feared Israel. But God spoke to Joshua with a promise before a single step was taken: "See, I have given into thine hand Jericho, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour" (Joshua 6:2, KJV).

Then God gave the strangest battle plan in the Bible. The armed men were to march around the city once a day for six days, with seven priests carrying seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark of the covenant. And Joshua commanded the people to stay silent — not a shout, not a word — until the day he told them to shout (Joshua 6:10). On the seventh day, they were to march around the city seven times. Then the priests would blow the trumpets, and at Joshua's command all the people would shout with a great shout.

Joshua and Israel did exactly as God commanded. Six days of silent marching. On the seventh day, they rose early, marched around the city seven times, the trumpets blew — and Joshua said to the people: "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city" (Joshua 6:16, KJV). The people shouted with a great shout — and "the wall fell down flat" (Joshua 6:20, KJV). Israel went straight in and took the city, just as God had promised. And Rahab and her family were kept safe and brought out, just as Joshua had promised — because she had hidden Israel's spies and trusted in Israel's God (Joshua 6:22–25).

The Point: The wall did not fall because Israel marched well or shouted loudly. The wall fell because God had given the city, exactly as He promised. The marching and the silence and the shout were obedience — God did the miracle. That's the pattern all through the Bible: God promises, God provides the power, and His people walk in obedience and watch Him work. One caution: God's promise at Jericho was His promise to Joshua — for that city, at that time. It is not a promise that God will knock down every difficulty in our lives if we follow the right steps. What carries over to us is God's character: He keeps His word, and He can be trusted when we obey what He has actually said to us in His Word.

The Application: God's way sometimes looks strange to us — too quiet, too slow, too simple. Joshua's army must have wondered during six silent days. But delayed is not denied: on God's day, in God's way, the wall fell flat. When God asks us to do something His way, we obey fully and trust Him for the result — the victory is His to give.

Discussion Questions

  1. God said "I have given into thine hand Jericho" before the marching started. How does a promise like that change how we obey?
  2. Why do you think God told the people to be silent for six days? What is hard about waiting quietly on God?
  3. Who actually brought the wall down — the shout, or God? How do we know?
  4. Rahab was an outsider in Jericho, yet she and her family were saved. What had she done, and what does that show about who God saves?
  5. What is one thing God asks of you that seems strange or hard — where you need to obey first and trust Him for the result?

Hands-On Activity — "Seven Laps and a Shout" (10 minutes)

Set the block wall on a table. The group marches quietly around the room six times (silent, like Israel). On the "seventh day," march seven more times (or walk the room once, counting seven laps of a smaller circle for space). Then, on the leader's signal, everyone shouts — and the leader knocks the block wall flat. Say: "Our shout didn't knock it down — hands did that part for our picture. In the real story, no hands touched the wall. God did it. The people just obeyed and watched."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, You keep every promise You make. Thank You for giving Israel the victory at Jericho. Help us to obey You fully and to trust You while we wait. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Joshua 6 together (younger children: verses 1–5, 15–20). Build a family "wall" of blocks or pillows, march around it seven times, and shout at the end. Then talk about one promise of God your family is trusting right now. Memory verse for the week: "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city" (Joshua 6:16, KJV).
