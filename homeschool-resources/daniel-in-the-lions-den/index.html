Daniel Prayed Anyway (Daniel in the Lions' Den)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Daniel 6 (KJV)

Big Idea: God is faithful — He delivers those who trust Him, so we can pray to Him no matter what.

Memory Verse: "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee." — Daniel 6:16 (KJV)

You Will Need: Paper and crayons for a "prayer window" card, Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Would You Stop?" (5 minutes)

Say: "Imagine a new law: nobody is allowed to pray — not at home, not anywhere — for thirty days. If you pray where anyone can see, you'll be in big trouble. Would you stop praying? Would you hide? Daniel faced exactly that law. Let's see what he did."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

Daniel served King Darius in Babylon, and the king trusted him so much that he planned to set Daniel over the whole kingdom. The other leaders were jealous, and they looked for something wrong in Daniel's work — but they could not find anything, because Daniel was faithful in everything.

So they made a trap out of Daniel's faith. They persuaded the king to sign a law: for thirty days, anyone who prayed to any god or man except the king would be thrown into the den of lions. The king signed it, and by the law of the Medes and Persians, it could not be changed.

What did Daniel do? The Bible says that when Daniel knew the law was signed, he went home, opened his windows toward Jerusalem, and prayed — just as he always had. "He kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime" (Daniel 6:10, KJV). Daniel didn't stop praying to stay safe. He simply kept doing what he had always done: he prayed to God.

The leaders caught him praying and brought him before the king. Darius was sorry — he tried all day to save Daniel — but the law could not be changed. As Daniel was thrown to the lions, the king said the words of our memory verse: "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee" (Daniel 6:16, KJV). A stone was laid over the mouth of the den and sealed. The king could not eat or sleep all night.

Very early in the morning, the king hurried to the den and cried out to Daniel. And Daniel answered! "My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me" (Daniel 6:22, KJV). Daniel was lifted out without a single wound, because he believed in his God.

Then King Darius wrote to all the people in his kingdom that everyone should tremble and fear before the God of Daniel: "for he is the living God, and stedfast for ever... He delivereth and rescueth, and he worketh signs and wonders in heaven and in earth, who hath delivered Daniel from the power of the lions" (Daniel 6:26–27, KJV).

The Point: Daniel was not rescued because he was lucky. He was rescued because God is faithful. God saw Daniel's trust, sent His angel, and shut the lions' mouths. The same God who delivered Daniel is alive today — "the living God, and stedfast for ever." That is why Daniel could pray without fear, and it is why we can too. And understand this clearly: God does not always deliver His people out of danger the way He delivered Daniel — when Daniel prayed that day, he did not know whether God would shut the lions' mouths. God remains just as faithful when He carries His people through hard things as when He delivers them out of them. Daniel trusted God either way — and so can we.

The Application: Daniel prayed three times a day, every day, before there was ever a law against it. His faithfulness in ordinary days carried him through the dangerous day. We can build the same habit: pray every day, thank God every day, and keep praying even when it's hard or unpopular. God hears, and God is faithful.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why couldn't the leaders find anything wrong with Daniel's work? What does "faithful" mean?
  2. Daniel prayed "as he did aforetime" — just like always. Why is a daily habit of prayer important before hard times come?
  3. The king couldn't save Daniel, but God could. What does that show us about God's power compared to any person's?
  4. Daniel gave thanks in his prayers (6:10), even with the lions' den ahead of him. What can you thank God for today?
  5. Is there a time when doing the right thing might be unpopular? How does Daniel's story help you?

Hands-On Activity — "Daniel's Prayer Window" (10 minutes)

Each child draws an open window on a card. Inside the window, they write or draw three things: one thing to thank God for, one person to pray for, and one need to bring to God — one for each of Daniel's three prayer times (morning, midday, evening). Say: "Daniel prayed at his window three times a day. Put your card by a window at home, and let it remind you to pray like Daniel — every day, no matter what."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, You are the living God, faithful forever. Thank You for delivering Daniel and for hearing our prayers. Help us to pray every day and to trust You no matter what. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Pick one day this week and pray together three times as a family — morning, midday, and evening — just as Daniel did. Keep it simple: thanks, a prayer for someone else, and a need. Talk afterward: was it easy or hard to stop three times? Memory verse for the week: "Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee" (Daniel 6:16, KJV).
