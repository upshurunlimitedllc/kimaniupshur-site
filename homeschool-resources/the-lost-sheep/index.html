Joy in Heaven (The Lost Sheep)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Luke 15:1–7 (KJV)

Big Idea: God rejoices over one sinner who repents — He seeks the lost, and heaven celebrates when one is found.

Memory Verse: "Likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth." — Luke 15:7 (KJV)

You Will Need: One cotton ball or small soft "sheep" to hide, Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Ninety-Nine and One" (5 minutes)

Say: "A shepherd has one hundred sheep. At the end of the day he counts: ninety-seven, ninety-eight, ninety-nine... one is missing. It's getting dark. The hills are dangerous. What should he do — stay safe with the ninety-nine, or go out after the one?" Let the kids argue it out. "Jesus told this exact story. Let's hear what His shepherd did."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

Publicans and sinners — people everyone else looked down on — were drawing near to hear Jesus. The Pharisees and scribes murmured against Him: "This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them" (Luke 15:2, KJV). So Jesus answered them with a story.

"What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?" (Luke 15:4, KJV). The shepherd goes after the one — not for a little while, but until he finds it. "And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing" (Luke 15:5, KJV). He doesn't scold the sheep. He carries it home on his shoulders, rejoicing, and calls his friends and neighbours together: "Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost" (Luke 15:6, KJV).

Then Jesus explained the story Himself: "I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance" (Luke 15:7, KJV).

The Point: This parable shows us the heart of God. God does not wait for lost people to find their own way back — He goes after them, and He keeps going until He finds them. And when one sinner repents and is found, heaven doesn't just approve — heaven rejoices. One person matters that much to God. The Pharisees complained that Jesus received sinners; Jesus answered that receiving sinners is exactly what God delights to do. Keep both halves of the story together: God's mercy does the seeking — and the sinner who is found repents. Heaven's joy is over "one sinner that repenteth": God's seeking mercy and our repentance are not rivals; the Shepherd seeks, and the lost come home by turning to Him.

The Application: If you have wandered from God — through disobedience, through drifting, through choices you regret — you are not too far for the Shepherd. He seeks, He finds, He carries, and heaven rejoices. And if you are one of His sheep, learn His heart: care about the lost people around you, pray for them by name, and rejoice — don't murmur — when one of them comes home.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why did the Pharisees murmur about Jesus? What did they think God was like? What did Jesus show them God is really like?
  2. The shepherd searched "until he find it." What does that tell us about God's patience with lost people?
  3. When the shepherd found the sheep, what did he do — and what did he not do? (He carried it, rejoicing; he didn't scold or punish it.)
  4. Jesus says there is more joy in heaven over one repenting sinner than over ninety-nine who need no repentance. Why is one found sinner such a big celebration?
  5. Who is one "lost sheep" you can pray for by name this week?

Hands-On Activity — "Seek Until Found, Then Rejoice" (10 minutes)

Hide the cotton-ball "sheep" in the room. One child is the shepherd and searches (the group may call "warmer/colder"). When the sheep is found, the shepherd carries it back on their shoulder — and the whole group does what the shepherd's friends did: they rejoice together (a cheer, a clap, a short "found!" song). Play several rounds. Say: "That's what heaven is like when one sinner repents — a celebration."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, thank You for seeking the lost and for the joy in heaven when one sinner repents. Thank You for finding us. Help us to care about lost people the way You do. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Luke 15:1–7 together. Each family member chooses one person who doesn't know Jesus and commits to praying for them by name every day this week. If your family knows someone who has recently come to faith, celebrate it together — heaven does. Memory verse for the week: "Likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth" (Luke 15:7, KJV).
