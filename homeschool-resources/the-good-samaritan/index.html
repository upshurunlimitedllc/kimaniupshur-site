Who Is My Neighbour? (The Good Samaritan)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Luke 10:25–37 (KJV)

Big Idea: God is merciful, and He teaches us that our neighbour is anyone in need whom we can help — love shows itself in action.

Memory Verse: "Go, and do thou likewise." — Luke 10:37 (KJV)

You Will Need: A few adhesive bandages and a small card per child (for the hands-on activity), Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Who Is My Neighbour?" (5 minutes)

Ask: "Who is your neighbour?" Let the kids answer (the people next door, on our street). Say: "A man once asked Jesus that exact question — and Jesus answered with a story that surprised everybody. Let's hear it."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

A lawyer stood up and asked Jesus what he must do to inherit eternal life. Jesus asked him what the law said, and the man answered: "Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy strength, and with all thy mind; and thy neighbour as thyself" (Luke 10:27, KJV). Jesus told him he had answered right. But the man, wanting to justify himself, asked: "And who is my neighbour?" (Luke 10:29, KJV).

So Jesus told this story. A man was traveling from Jerusalem to Jericho when thieves attacked him — they stripped him, wounded him, and left him half dead. A priest happened to come down that road; when he saw the man, he passed by on the other side. A Levite came, looked at him, and also passed by on the other side.

Then a Samaritan came where he was — and Samaritans and Jews were enemies in those days, who had no dealings with each other. But when the Samaritan saw the wounded man, "he had compassion on him" (Luke 10:33, KJV). He went to him, bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine, set him on his own beast, brought him to an inn, and took care of him. The next day he gave the innkeeper two pence and said: take care of him, and whatever more you spend, when I come again, I will repay you.

Then Jesus asked the lawyer: "Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour unto him that fell among the thieves?" The man answered: "He that shewed mercy on him." And Jesus said: "Go, and do thou likewise" (Luke 10:36–37, KJV).

The Point: Jesus turned the question around. The lawyer asked, "Who qualifies as my neighbour?" Jesus answered by showing what a neighbour does: a neighbour shows mercy. God's own heart is mercy — He saw us in our need and came to help us at His own cost. The Samaritan's mercy pictures that heart: he stopped, he came close, he gave his own oil, his own animal, his own money. Love for God and love for neighbour are not two separate things; the second proves the first.

The Application: Our neighbour is not only the person next door. Our neighbour is anyone God puts in front of us who needs help — the classmate sitting alone, the new kid, the person being teased, the family member who is sad. And love is not just a feeling: it stops, it helps, and sometimes it costs us something. Jesus's command is simple: "Go, and do thou likewise."

Discussion Questions

  1. What did the priest and the Levite do when they saw the man? (The Bible does not tell us their reasons.) What excuses do we sometimes make for not helping?
  2. The Samaritan and the wounded man were from peoples who were enemies. Why does that make the Samaritan's mercy even more powerful?
  3. What did the Samaritan's help cost him? (His time, his oil and wine, his animal, his money.)
  4. Jesus said, "He that shewed mercy on him" was the neighbour. So who is your neighbour, by Jesus's definition?
  5. Who is someone in your life right now who needs mercy shown to them? What could you actually do?

Hands-On Activity — "Neighbour Helper Cards" (10 minutes)

Give each child a blank card and bandages. On the card, each child writes: "I can be a neighbour. This week I will help ___ by ___." (Fill in a real person and a real action.) Decorate the card and stick one bandage on it as a reminder of the Samaritan, who bound up wounds. Say: "Mercy isn't a feeling — it's what we do. This week, do what you wrote."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, thank You for Your mercy to us. Open our eyes to see the people who need help, and give us hearts that stop and show mercy, like the Samaritan. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Luke 10:25–37 together. As a family, choose one act of mercy for this week — visit someone lonely, help a neighbour with a chore, give to someone in need — and do it together. Talk about what it cost and what it meant. Memory verse for the week: "Go, and do thou likewise" (Luke 10:37, KJV).
