The Boy Who Shared His Lunch

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12)

Scripture: John 6:1–14 (KJV); see also Matthew 14:21

Big Idea: Jesus is the Son of God, and He has power to provide.

Memory Verse: "God loveth a cheerful giver." — 2 Corinthians 9:7 (KJV)

You Will Need: A basket or bag, five paper circles (the "loaves") and two paper fish shapes (optional, for the opening activity), Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Is It Enough?" (5 minutes)

Hold up the basket with the five paper loaves and two paper fish. Say: "Imagine a crowd of thousands of hungry people — more people than fill a whole stadium. And the only food anyone can find is this: one boy's lunch. Do you think it's enough?" Let the kids answer. "Today we'll see what Jesus did — because this story is really about who Jesus is."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

After Jesus had been teaching all day, a huge crowd was hungry, and there was no shop nearby to buy food for everyone. Jesus asked His disciple Philip where they could buy bread, but Philip knew it would take more money than they had.

Then Andrew, another disciple, spoke up. He had found a boy in the crowd who had some food: "There is a lad here, which hath five barley loaves, and two small fishes: but what are they among so many?" (John 6:9, KJV). The Bible doesn't tell us how the boy felt about his lunch being mentioned — it simply tells us the lunch was there, and that Jesus took it from there.

Here is what Jesus did: "Jesus took the loaves; and when he had given thanks, he distributed to the disciples, and the disciples to them that were set down; and likewise of the fishes as much as they would" (John 6:11, KJV). The people ate as much as they wanted — and they were all filled. Then Jesus said, "Gather up the fragments that remain, that nothing be lost" (John 6:12, KJV), and the disciples filled twelve baskets with the pieces that were left over.

How many people ate that day? John's Gospel tells us about five thousand men — and Matthew's account adds "beside women and children" (Matthew 14:21, KJV). Thousands and thousands of people, fed from five small loaves and two small fish.

When the people saw what Jesus had done, they said, "This is of a truth that prophet that should come into the world" (John 6:14, KJV).

The Point: This miracle happened because of who Jesus is. Jesus is the Son of God, and He has power to provide. The lunch didn't feed the crowd — Jesus did. He gave thanks to His Father, and by His own power He multiplied the bread and the fish until everyone was full, with twelve baskets left over. When we are in need, we look to Jesus first, because He is able to provide.

The Application: Jesus was not limited by how little food there was, and He still chose to use what was at hand. That is good news for us: we can bring what we have to God — our time, our help, our kind words, what we own — and trust Him with it. Our giving doesn't make God powerful; God is powerful already. Giving is simply how we take part in what He is doing, and we can give cheerfully, because "God loveth a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7, KJV).

Discussion Questions

  1. Andrew said, "but what are they among so many?" Why do you think he said that? Have you ever seen a need that looked way too big?
  2. What does this miracle show us about who Jesus is? (Guide the kids to the main truth: Jesus is the Son of God, and He has power to provide.)
  3. The Bible doesn't tell us the boy offered his lunch or how he felt about it — it tells us what Jesus did with it. Why is it important to stick to what the Bible actually says?
  4. Jesus gave thanks before anyone had eaten. What can we thank God for, even before we see how He will provide?
  5. What are some things you can bring to God and share with others this week — your time, your help, your kind words, part of something you own?

Review Game — "Pass the Basket" (5–10 minutes)

Sit in a circle. Pass the basket around while you play music or clap a rhythm. When the music stops, the child holding the basket answers a quick review question (How many loaves? How many fish? Who gave thanks? How many baskets were left over? What did the people say about Jesus?). Keep it fast and cheerful — everyone wins when the whole circle can answer together.

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, thank You that Jesus is Your Son, and that He has power to provide for us. Help us to trust You with everything we have, and to give cheerfully. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Note for Parents

Today your child learned from John 6 that Jesus, the Son of God, fed thousands by His own power — and that we can trust Him to provide. Ask them: "What is one thing you can share with someone this week?" Memory verse: "God loveth a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7, KJV).
