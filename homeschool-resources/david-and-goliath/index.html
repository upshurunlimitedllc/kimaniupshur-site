David Trusted God (David and Goliath)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: 1 Samuel 17 (KJV)

Big Idea: God is mighty, and He fights for His people — we can trust His power, not our own strength.

Memory Verse: "For the battle is the LORD'S." — 1 Samuel 17:47 (KJV)

You Will Need: Five smooth stones (or five paper stone shapes), a small bag, Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Too Big?" (5 minutes)

Hold up the five paper stones in the bag. Say: "A giant soldier, over nine feet tall, in full armor, is daring anyone to fight him. The whole army is afraid. All that comes to meet him is a young shepherd with a sling and five stones. Who would you bet on?" Let the kids answer. "Today we'll see why David wasn't afraid — because David knew who was really fighting."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

Israel's army faced the Philistines, and every morning and evening for forty days a giant named Goliath came out and dared Israel to send a man to fight him. King Saul and all the soldiers were afraid, and no one went.

David was the youngest of eight brothers. He kept his father's sheep, and he had come to the camp only to bring food to his older brothers. But when David heard Goliath defying Israel, he also heard something else: Goliath was defying the armies of the living God.

David remembered what God had already done for him. He told King Saul: "The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine" (1 Samuel 17:37, KJV). Saul offered David his armor, but David could not use it — he had not tested it. Instead David took his staff, chose five smooth stones from the brook, put them in his shepherd's bag, and went out with his sling.

Goliath mocked him. David answered with the most important words in the story: "Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied" (1 Samuel 17:45, KJV). And David said why he was sure: "for the battle is the LORD'S, and he will give you into our hands" (1 Samuel 17:47, KJV).

David ran toward Goliath, took a stone, slung it, and struck the giant on the forehead — and Goliath fell face down to the ground. When the Philistines saw their champion was defeated, they fled, and Israel won the victory that day.

The Point: This story is not mainly about a brave boy. It is about a mighty God. David was not trusting his sling, his stones, or his own aim — he said so himself: he came in the name of the LORD. God had delivered David from the lion and the bear, and David trusted the same God to deliver him again. The battle belonged to the Lord, and the Lord won it. One thing to understand clearly: trusting God does not mean God promises to win every hard thing for us in the way we hope — His people sometimes face difficulties He does not take away. What David's story teaches is that God is worthy of our trust whatever the outcome, because He is mighty, He is faithful, and He is with His people.

The Application: We all face things that look too big — a hard test, a bully, a fear we can't shake. Courage is not pretending we're strong. Courage is remembering who God is and what He has already done, and trusting Him. Like David, we can say: the battle is the LORD'S.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why was everyone in Israel's army afraid of Goliath? What did David see that they were missing?
  2. What had God already done for David (the lion and the bear)? How did remembering that help him?
  3. David said, "I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts." What does it mean to face something hard in the Lord's name instead of our own strength?
  4. Saul's armor didn't fit David. What does that teach us about trusting God in the way He has prepared us, instead of copying someone else?
  5. What is one "giant" in your life where you need to remember that the battle is the LORD'S?

Hands-On Activity — "Five Stones of Trust" (10 minutes)

Give each child five paper stones. On each stone, they write or draw one true thing about God or one way God has helped them (God is mighty · God hears prayer · God saved David · God loves me · God never leaves me). Put the stones in the bag. Say: "David carried five stones, but his trust wasn't in the stones — it was in God. Keep your stones this week. When something feels too big, pick one up and read it."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, You are mighty, and the battle is Yours. Thank You for delivering David, and for being the same God today. Help us to trust Your power and not our own strength. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read 1 Samuel 17 together this week (younger children: verses 32–50). Then make a family "Trust List": write down three hard things God has already brought your family through, and pray together about one big thing you are trusting Him with now. Memory verse for the week: "For the battle is the LORD'S" (1 Samuel 17:47, KJV).
