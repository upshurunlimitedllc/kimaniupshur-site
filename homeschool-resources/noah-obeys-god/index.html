Noah Did All God Said (Noah and the Ark)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Genesis 6–8 (KJV); the rainbow promise, Genesis 9:13

Big Idea: God is holy and judges sin — and God is gracious: He saves those who trust Him and obey Him.

Memory Verse: "Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he." — Genesis 6:22 (KJV)

You Will Need: Building materials for a tabletop "ark" (blocks, cardboard, or paper), a small toy boat if available, Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "Follow Every Instruction" (5 minutes)

Give the group a silly building task with exact steps: "Put down three blocks. Now two on top. Now one across — not four steps, not two — every step, in order." When it's done, ask: "What if you'd skipped a step? Today we'll meet a man who followed God's instructions for the biggest building project in the Bible — and it saved his whole family."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

In Noah's day, the earth had filled with wickedness and violence, and God was grieved by the sin of mankind. But there was one man who walked with God: "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD" (Genesis 6:8, KJV).

God told Noah that He would send a flood to judge the earth, and He gave Noah a rescue plan: build an ark — a huge boat — of gopher wood, with rooms, pitched inside and out, built to the exact size God gave. Noah was to bring his family into the ark, and two of every kind of animal (and seven of the clean animals), and food for them all.

Noah believed God — and Noah obeyed. The Bible's summary of his whole life of work is our memory verse: "Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he" (Genesis 6:22, KJV). Noah didn't obey partway. He did all that God commanded.

Then the rain came — forty days and forty nights — and the waters rose until they covered the mountains. Everything outside the ark perished. But God had not forgotten the family inside: "And God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the cattle that was with him in the ark" (Genesis 8:1, KJV). God sent a wind, the waters went down, and in time Noah sent out a dove, which came back with an olive leaf in her mouth — the earth was drying. At God's command, Noah brought his family and the animals out of the ark, and the first thing Noah did was build an altar and worship God.

Then God made a promise, and He set a sign for it in the sky: "I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth" (Genesis 9:13, KJV). Every rainbow since that day is a reminder of God's promise.

The Point: This story shows us two true things about God at the same time. God is holy: He does not shrug at sin; He judges it. And God is gracious: before the flood ever came, God provided a way of rescue, and He remembered Noah through the whole storm. Noah was saved not because he was strong or clever, but because God gave grace — and Noah responded the right way: he believed God, and he obeyed Him completely. The order matters: Noah's obedience did not earn God's grace. "Noah found grace" (Genesis 6:8) comes first — grace was God's gift, and Noah's building and obeying were his response to it, not his payment for it.

The Application: Obedience is faith with its sleeves rolled up. Noah trusted God's word about things he had never seen — and he built. We obey God the same way: not halfway, not only when it's easy, but all that He commands, because the God who commands is the God who saves. And when storms come, we can remember: God remembered Noah — and God remembers His people.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why did Noah "find grace in the eyes of the LORD"? What is grace?
  2. Noah obeyed "all that God commanded." Is halfway obedience really obedience? Why or why not?
  3. What does "God remembered Noah" mean? (Not that God forgot — that God acted for Noah's good.) How is that a comfort in hard times?
  4. What was the first thing Noah did when he left the ark? What does that teach us about gratitude?
  5. What does the rainbow remind us of? How is God's promise-keeping different from people's?

Hands-On Activity — "Build the Ark — Every Step" (10–15 minutes)

In pairs or small groups, build a tabletop ark from the materials, following a short list of steps in order (base, walls, roof, door on the side). When each ark is done, read Genesis 6:22 aloud together. Say: "Your ark only worked because you followed the steps. Noah's obedience worked the same way — he did all that God commanded him. God gave the plan, God gave the grace — Noah gave the obedience."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, You are holy, and You are gracious. Thank You for remembering Noah, and for remembering us. Help us to believe Your word and obey You completely, like Noah did. In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Genesis 6:22 and 8:1 together, and talk about one instruction from God your family sometimes obeys "halfway." Choose to do it fully this week. If a rainbow appears, stop and remember Genesis 9:13 together. Memory verse for the week: "Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he" (Genesis 6:22, KJV).
