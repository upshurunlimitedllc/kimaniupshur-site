He Is Risen (The Empty Tomb)

A Bible Lesson for Kids (Ages 6–12) · Upshur Unlimited LLC Bible Lesson Library

Scripture: Luke 24:1–12 (KJV); see also Matthew 28:5–6

Big Idea: Jesus rose from the dead, just as He said — He is alive, and He has conquered sin and death.

Memory Verse: "He is not here, but is risen." — Luke 24:6 (KJV)

You Will Need: A large paper circle (the "stone") and a chair or box (the "tomb") for the opening activity, Bibles for older kids.

Opening Activity — "The Stone Is Moved" (5 minutes)

Set the paper "stone" against the "tomb." Say: "Jesus had died on the cross, and His body was laid in a tomb with a great stone rolled against the door — Matthew tells us the tomb was made secure, sealed, and guarded (Matthew 27:66). Early on the third morning, some women who loved Jesus came to the tomb with spices. What do you think they expected to find?" Let the kids answer. "What they found changed the world."

Bible Teaching (10–15 minutes)

Very early in the morning on the first day of the week, the women came to the tomb, bringing the spices they had prepared for Jesus's body. But they found the stone rolled away from the tomb. They went in — and they did not find the body of the Lord Jesus.

As they stood there, perplexed, two men stood by them in shining garments. The women were afraid and bowed their faces to the earth. The angels said to them: "Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee, saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again" (Luke 24:5–7, KJV). And the women remembered Jesus's words.

They ran from the tomb and told all these things to the eleven apostles and the others. But the apostles thought their words were idle tales, and they did not believe them. Peter, however, ran to the tomb. He stooped down, saw the linen clothes laid by themselves — and went away, wondering what had happened.

The Point: The empty tomb is the heart of the Christian faith. Jesus did not merely survive, and His body was not hidden or stolen — He rose, exactly as He had promised He would. Death could not hold the Son of God. That is why the angels' question is so powerful: "Why seek ye the living among the dead?" Jesus is not a memory. He is alive — risen — and because He lives, sin is forgiven and death is defeated for all who trust in Him. Notice carefully what Luke tells us in these verses: the women saw the empty tomb and heard the two men's message, and Peter saw the tomb and the linen clothes — the women did not see Jesus Himself that morning. Jesus appeared to His followers later that same day, as Luke tells us next (Luke 24:13–35). The women's testimony came first, and it was true — even when the apostles did not believe it at first.

The Application: The women remembered Jesus's words, believed, and ran to tell. Peter wondered, and ran to see. We respond the same way: remember what Jesus said, believe what God has done, and tell others the best news ever heard — He is not here; He is risen. Easter is not the end of a sad story. It is the morning everything changed.

Discussion Questions

  1. What did the women expect to find at the tomb? What did they actually find?
  2. The angels said, "remember how he spake unto you." Why is remembering Jesus's words so important when we're confused or sad?
  3. The apostles first thought the women's report was an "idle tale." Peter ran to look anyway. What can we learn from Peter?
  4. What does Jesus's resurrection prove about who He is? Why would Christianity be empty without it?
  5. The women ran and told others. Who can you tell this week that Jesus is risen?

Hands-On Activity — "Roll the Stone Away" (10 minutes)

Each child draws a tomb on paper: a hill, a doorway, and a round stone. They draw the stone rolled away to the side and write the angels' words above the empty doorway: "He is not here, but is risen" (Luke 24:6). Younger children can add a sunrise behind the hill. Say: "Every picture of the empty tomb is a picture of a promise kept. Jesus said He would rise — and He did."

Closing Prayer

"Dear God, thank You that Jesus is alive — that the tomb is empty and death is defeated. Help us to remember His words, believe the good news, and tell others: He is risen! In Jesus' name, amen."

Take-Home Family Activity

Read Luke 24:1–12 together. Then go outside at sunrise or early morning one day this week, and read the angels' words aloud as a family: "He is not here, but is risen." Talk about one person each of you will tell. Memory verse for the week: "He is not here, but is risen" (Luke 24:6, KJV).
