Knowing God: 5 Days in His Character

By Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

A 5-day Bible reading plan · English · Scripture: King James Version (KJV) · Free to read — no email, no signup.

By Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

About These Five Days

Eternal life, Jesus said, is knowing the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom He sent. Not knowing about Him — knowing Him. There is a difference, and it is the difference between facts held at a distance and a relationship lived up close.

These five days walk through who God is, as He reveals Himself in His own words. Day 1 meets the God who wants to be known. Day 2 hears the Father describe His own heart: merciful and gracious. Day 3 looks at God made visible in Jesus Christ, full of grace and truth. Day 4 welcomes the Holy Spirit, who teaches us and lives within us. Day 5 rests on the God who keeps every promise He makes.

"And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent."

— John 17:3 (KJV)

All Scripture quotations in this plan are from the King James Version (KJV).

Day 1: The God Who Wants to Be Known

Scripture: Jeremiah 9:23-24; Jeremiah 24:7; Jeremiah 29:13

"Thus saith the Lord, Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, neither let the mighty man glory in his might, let not the rich man glory in his riches: But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me, that I am the Lord which exercise lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness, in the earth: for in these things I delight, saith the Lord."

— Jeremiah 9:23-24 (KJV)

"And I will give them an heart to know me, that I am the Lord: and they shall be my people, and I will be their God: for they shall return unto me with their whole heart."

— Jeremiah 24:7 (KJV)

"And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart."

— Jeremiah 29:13 (KJV)

There is a difference between knowing about God and knowing God. Facts about Him can be memorized the way facts from a history lesson are memorized: He knows all things, He can do all things, He is everywhere at once. A person can hold every one of those facts and still never have walked with Him through one ordinary afternoon. Scripture never treats knowing God as a test to pass. It treats it as a relationship to live.

That is why the words from Jeremiah land with such weight. God Himself says what a person should glory in: not wisdom, not strength, not riches, but this — that he understands and knows Me. The God who speaks here is not distant. He names what He delights in: lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness in the earth. He is telling us what He is like, and He is inviting us to know Him as He is.

Notice, too, who makes the first move. God promises to give His people a heart to know Him. He does not wait for us to climb up to Him; He comes toward us. In the garden, after Adam and Eve had sinned and hidden themselves, it was God who came walking and calling, "Where art thou?" (Genesis 3:9). The seeking began with Him then, and it begins with Him now.

His promise is plain: seek Me with all your heart, and you will find Me. Not maybe. Not if you are impressive enough. Wholehearted seeking is answered, because the One being sought wants to be found. Halfhearted curiosity finds little, the way a friendship fades when no time is ever made for it. But the door is open, and you do not have to make yourself worthy before you knock. You only have to come honestly, with your whole heart, to the God who has already come toward you.

Prayer

Father, You said You would give me a heart to know You, and I receive it. I do not want facts about You held at a distance; I want to know You. Teach me who You are. Draw me with my whole heart, and let me find You, as You promised. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 2: Merciful and Gracious — The Father in His Own Words

Scripture: Exodus 34:6-7; James 1:17

"And the Lord passed by before him, and proclaimed, The Lord, The Lord God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth,"

— Exodus 34:6 (KJV)

"Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin,"

— Exodus 34:7 (KJV)

"Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning."

— James 1:17 (KJV)

When Moses asked to see God's glory, God answered by proclaiming His own name — His character, in His own words. Of everything God could have said first about Himself, He said this: merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth. Mercy first. Grace first. Patience beyond what we deserve. This is not a description someone else wrote about God. It is God's own description of Himself, and it is where knowing Him must begin.

Jesus taught His followers to speak to God as Father — not as a distant ruler or an impersonal force, but as Father. For some, that word is hard to hear, because an earthly father was absent, harsh, or frightening. Hear this plainly: God is not a larger version of an earthly father who fell short. He is the Father every earthly father was meant to picture. Present. Protective. Tender. Just. Delighting in His children.

And He does not change with moods or seasons. Every good gift comes down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variableness, no shifting, no shadow of turning. The mercy He proclaimed to Moses is the mercy He shows today. Nothing about His goodness depends on the weather of your week.

He also keeps mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin. That sentence carries the whole story of redemption in seed form: a holy God who does not pretend sin is small, and a merciful God who forgives it fully. Knowing the Father means holding both truths together — His goodness is abundant, and His forgiveness is real. Come to Him the way a child comes to a father who is glad he came. He is not annoyed that you are here. He has never turned away a heart that came honestly.

Prayer

Father, thank You that mercy and grace are the first words of Your own description. Thank You that You do not change, and that Your goodness does not run out. Heal every place where I have pictured You as harsh or far away. Let me know You as You truly are — abundant in goodness and truth. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 3: Full of Grace and Truth — God Seen in Jesus Christ

Scripture: John 1:14; John 3:16; Hebrews 7:25

"And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth."

— John 1:14 (KJV)

"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."

— John 3:16 (KJV)

"Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."

— Hebrews 7:25 (KJV)

If you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus. That is the plain claim of Scripture: the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth. God did not send a description of Himself from a distance. He came. In Jesus, the invisible God became visible — His compassion for crowds, His tears at a friend's grave, His patience with slow learners all show the Father's heart in action. To study Jesus is to study God.

John 3:16 tells us why He came: God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son. The cross is where the character of God shines brightest. There, God's justice and God's mercy met in one act: sin was borne and paid for by Jesus Himself, who gave His life a ransom for many (Mark 10:45), and sinners were given everlasting life instead of perishing. Knowing God is not complete until you know Him here — as the Redeemer who delivers His people out of sin and brings them to Himself. Our part is not to earn it. Our part is to believe in the Son He gave, and to receive what His finished work provides.

And Jesus is not finished being known. He rose. He lives. He is able to save to the uttermost those who come to God by Him, because He ever lives to make intercession for them. Right now, the Son who died for you also prays for you. He is Savior, Lord, Friend, and Shepherd — each name a doorway into knowing Him more. He is not known by admiring Him from a distance. He is known by following Him, trusting His words, and resting in what He has done.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for coming near enough to be seen, and for giving Your life so that sinners could be saved to the uttermost. I rest in what You have done. Because You live and pray for me, I come to the Father through You with confidence. Reveal the Father to me as I follow You. In Your name, Amen.

Day 4: The Holy Spirit — The Teacher Who Lives Within

Scripture: John 14:26; Galatians 5:22-23

"But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you."

— John 14:26 (KJV)

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law."

— Galatians 5:22-23 (KJV)

Many believers think of the Holy Spirit the way they think of a distant relative — real, but not really known. That is a great loss, because the Holy Spirit is the One who makes knowing God personal and present. The Father planned our salvation. The Son accomplished it. The Spirit applies it — teaching, reminding, and living within.

Jesus called Him the Comforter, sent by the Father in the Son's name. He is not a force or a feeling. He is God, the third Person of the Trinity, who speaks, teaches, guides, and can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30). His work in a believer is wide and kind. He gives new birth. He takes up residence within, so that the believer's body is the temple of the Holy Ghost (1 Corinthians 6:19). He opens the Scriptures so that hearing becomes understanding. He convicts of sin — never to crush, always to cleanse and make whole. He helps our weakest prayers, making intercession when words fail.

And He grows character. The fruit of the Spirit is not manufactured by effort; it is grown by abiding. Love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance — this is what God's own character looks like when the Spirit is forming it in a person. You cannot grit your teeth into gentleness. You yield to the Spirit, and He produces in you what you could never produce in yourself.

So speak to Him as a Person, because He is one. Welcome Him in the morning. Listen for His reminders of what Jesus said. Obey His quiet leading quickly and gladly. The God who seemed distant becomes near, because the Spirit is God's own presence with you — He does not come and go. He dwells.

Prayer

Holy Spirit, I welcome You. You live in me; teach me all things, and bring the words of Jesus back to my mind when I need them. Grow Your fruit in me — love, joy, and peace that I cannot manufacture. Lead me today, and make me quick to obey. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 5: The God Who Keeps His Promises

Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:20; Joshua 23:14; Hebrews 13:5

"For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us."

— 2 Corinthians 1:20 (KJV)

"...not one thing hath failed of all the good things which the Lord your God spake concerning you; all are come to pass unto you, and not one thing hath failed thereof."

— Joshua 23:14 (KJV)

"...for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee."

— Hebrews 13:5 (KJV)

You can trust God because He has a record. Scripture is, among other things, the documented history of God keeping His word — to individuals, to families, to a whole nation, across centuries. Joshua, at the end of his life, could testify that not one thing had failed of all the good things the Lord had spoken. Every promise had come to pass. Not one had failed.

Paul says the same of the whole story: all the promises of God in Christ are yea, and in Him Amen. Jesus is the living proof that God's word holds. The promise of a Savior was kept. The promise of the Spirit was kept. The promise of His presence is kept daily: I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee. And delay is not denial. Abraham waited years for the child God promised. Joseph waited years between the pit and the palace. God was never late in either story; He was working in the waiting.

Trust is built on proven faithfulness, the way trust in a friend is built — promise made, promise kept, again and again. That is why knowing God and trusting God grow together. The more of His record you know, the steadier your heart becomes when everything else shakes.

Hold His promises personally. He promises His presence, His peace, His wisdom to those who ask, His supply for your needs, and rest for the weary. Bring His own words back to Him in prayer, and stand on them. The God who kept every promise in this long record will keep every promise He has made to you. Knowing Him means you can count on Him — and counted on, He has never once failed.

Prayer

Faithful God, Your record is perfect: not one word has failed. I bring Your promises back to You, and I stand on them. When answers tarry, keep my heart steady in the waiting. You will never leave me nor forsake me, and that is enough. In Jesus' name, Amen.
