Salvation: God’s Free Gift of Eternal Life

By Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

A 7-day Bible reading plan · English · Scripture: King James Version (KJV) · Based on the book Salvation: By Grace, Through Faith by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed. · Free to read — no email, no signup.

About This Plan

If you died tonight, do you know where you would spend eternity? That is the most important question a person can answer — and God's Word does not treat it as a mystery. It treats it as a rescue. Over the next seven days, you will see from Scripture what salvation is, why every one of us needs it, what Jesus Christ did to provide it, and how you can receive it for yourself. Salvation is God's rescue of sinners, given as a gift, received by faith. Rescued. Redeemed. Restored. That is the story — and it can be your story.

Day 1 — God's Love for Humanity

Scripture Reading

"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." (John 3:16, KJV)

"But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us." (Romans 5:8, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

Everything about salvation starts with God — not with you. Before you ever thought about Him, He was thinking about you. Read John 3:16 slowly, word by word. For God — salvation begins with Him. So loved — not a casual fondness, but a love so deep it acted, it moved, it gave. The world — not the good people, not the religious people, but the whole world, including people who feel too far gone, and including you on your worst day. That he gave — real love gives, and God's giving cost Him everything: His only begotten Son. That whosoever believeth in him — whoever. No exceptions, no fine print. Should not perish, but have everlasting life — that is the promise, stated as a certainty.

Jesus first spoke these words to Nicodemus, a religious leader who had his life looking right on the outside but still needed what only God could give. Religion is man's reach toward God; John 3:16 is God's reach toward man.

Then notice when God loved you. Paul says Christ died for us "while we were yet sinners." God did not wait for you to clean up your act. He did not love some future, improved version of you. He loved you in your sin — and proved it by giving His Son. That order matters, because it destroys every idea that salvation is something you earn. You don't get loved and then get saved. You get loved first, and that love does the saving.

So as this week begins, settle this in your heart: the God of the Bible is not reluctant toward you. He is the Father who gave heaven's best for earth's worst. Whatever you have done, wherever you are starting from, His love has already moved in your direction. The gift is given. The rest of this week is about understanding it — and receiving it.

Reflection Question

When you read "For God so loved the world," do you include yourself in "the world"? What would change in your heart if you believed God loved you — personally, and first — before you ever did anything to deserve it?

Closing Prayer

Father in heaven, thank You for loving me before I ever loved You. Thank You that while I was still a sinner, You gave Your only begotten Son for me. Let Your love land personally on my heart today. I don't want to just know about Your love — I want to receive it. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 2 — Understanding Sin

Scripture Reading

"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God." (Romans 3:23, KJV)

"As it is written, There is none righteous, no, not one." (Romans 3:10, KJV)

"For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord." (Romans 6:23, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

Nobody enjoys hearing that they are a sinner. But you cannot appreciate a rescue until you admit you are in trouble, and God's diagnosis is plain: all have sinned. Not some. Not only the worst offenders. All. That includes the nicest person you know — and it includes you and me.

It's tempting to answer, "But I'm a good person." Compared with a criminal, perhaps. But God's standard is not "better than average," and He doesn't grade on a curve. The comparison that matters is not between you and your neighbor; it's between you and a perfectly holy God — and against His glory, every one of us falls short. One lie, one moment of hatred, one thing put before God — sin starts in the heart, and every heart has known it.

Why does sin matter so much? Because sin has a price: "the wages of sin is death." Wages are what you earn, and what sin earns is death — spiritual death, separation from God. That isn't God being cruel; it's God being holy. And the problem runs deeper than individual deeds. We are not only sinners because we sin; Scripture shows we sin because sin is in our nature. That's why "trying harder" never fixes it — you can't solve a nature problem with a behavior program.

But here's the good news hiding inside the bad news: God's diagnosis always comes with God's cure. A doctor only tells you you're sick because he has the medicine. God shows us our sin not to crush us, but to drive us to the Savior. Jesus came for the sick, not for people who think they're healthy. The honest diagnosis is not the end of your story — it's the doorway into grace. Tomorrow we'll meet the Savior God sent. For today, let the truth do its kind work: we need rescuing — and a Rescuer has come.

Reflection Question

Have you been measuring yourself against other people instead of against God's holiness? What would it mean for you to say honestly to God today, "I am a sinner, and I can't save myself"?

Closing Prayer

Father, I admit what Your Word says: I have sinned and fallen short of Your glory. I can't fix my own heart, and I won't pretend otherwise. Thank You that You show me my sin because You have provided a Savior. Prepare my heart to receive Him. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 3 — Jesus Christ, Our Savior

Scripture Reading

"For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved." (John 3:17, KJV)

"And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent." (John 17:3, KJV)

"Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved." (Acts 4:12, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

Who is Jesus — and why did He have to be the one to come? The Bible's answer is that Jesus is fully the Son of God and fully the Son of Man. He is the eternal Son, sent by the Father; and He truly became one of us, born into our world, living the life we could not live. The Father didn't send an angel. He didn't send a prophet. He sent His Son — that's the measure of God's love, and it's why Jesus is not merely a good teacher or an inspiring example. He is the Savior.

Notice why He came. God did not send His Son into the world to condemn the world. Condemnation was already our problem; Jesus came to carry us out of it — "that the world through him might be saved." If you have pictured God standing over you with a pointed finger, John's Gospel corrects the picture: the Son came on a rescue mission.

And notice what He brings. Eternal life, Jesus says, is knowing God — "the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent." Salvation is not only escape from death; it is entrance into a relationship with God Himself. That's why no substitute will do. Acts declares there is salvation in no other, and no other name under heaven by which we must be saved. Every other system offers advice about reaching God. Jesus offers Himself — God reaching down to us.

This is also why the book behind this plan insists on the whole God of the Bible at work in your salvation: God the Father, who loved the world and gave His Son; Jesus Christ, who died, was buried, and rose again to pay for our sins; and the Holy Spirit, who convicts us of sin and gives us new birth. Tomorrow we stand at the cross and the empty tomb — the center of everything Jesus came to do.

Reflection Question

Have you thought of Jesus mainly as a teacher or example, rather than as the Son of God sent to save you personally? What does it mean to you that He came not to condemn you, but so that you "might be saved"?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, I believe You are the Son of God, sent by the Father — not to condemn me, but to save me. Thank You for coming into this world for people who could not come to You. Help me to know You, not just know about You. In Your name I pray, Amen.

Day 4 — The Cross and the Resurrection

Scripture Reading

"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed." (Isaiah 53:5, KJV)

"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed." (1 Peter 2:24, KJV)

"For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures." (1 Corinthians 15:3-4, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

This is the center of everything. On the cross, Jesus Himself — in His own body — bore our sins. Not a symbol. Not someone else's punishment. Every lie, every lustful thought, every act of hatred, every moment of pride: placed on Him. Isaiah saw it centuries before it happened: He was wounded for our transgressions, bruised for our iniquities. The punishment was ours; He took it. On that cross, God treated Jesus as if He had lived our sinful lives — so that He can treat us as if we had lived Jesus' perfect life. Your sin for His righteousness. That's the great exchange.

Why did it have to be this way? Because God is both loving and just. His love wants to forgive; His justice requires that sin be dealt with. The cross is where love and justice meet: God punished sin fully — in Jesus — so that He can forgive sinners fully, and justly.

But the cross is not the end of the story. Paul defines the gospel itself with three facts of first importance: Christ died for our sins, He was buried, and He rose again the third day. The resurrection proves it all worked. A dead savior saves no one — but Jesus walked out of that tomb, defeating sin, death, and the grave. Because He lives, everyone who trusts Him will live too.

And hear His last word from the cross: "It is finished" (John 19:30, KJV). Not "I'm finished" — it is finished. The payment is complete. The debt is paid in full. There is nothing left for you to add and nothing left for you to earn. Your part is not to finish the work. Your part is to receive the finished work.

Reflection Question

Do you ever feel you need to add something to what Jesus did — extra effort, extra penance, extra proof? What would it mean to rest today in two words: "It is finished"?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for bearing my sins in Your body on the cross. Thank You for dying, being buried, and rising again for me. Your work is finished, and I receive it. I stop trying to pay a debt You have already paid in full. In Your name, Amen.

Day 5 — Saved by Grace Through Faith

Scripture Reading

"For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast." (Ephesians 2:8-9, KJV)

"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." (Matthew 11:28, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

Three words carry the whole gospel: grace, saved, gift. Grace is God's undeserved favor. Saved means rescued from something deadly. And a gift is something you receive, not something you earn. Put them together and you have the Bible's plain definition: salvation is God's rescue of sinners, given as a gift, received by faith.

Picture a man drowning in the ocean. He can't save himself — he's going down. Salvation is the lifeline thrown to him. He doesn't earn the lifeline. He doesn't help make the rope. His only part is to grab hold. That's faith: taking hold of what God has already done for you in Jesus Christ.

Why does God insist it be a gift — "not of works, lest any man should boast"? Because if works contributed even a little, heaven would be full of people comparing résumés. If you could earn it, it wouldn't be grace. Imagine you owed an impossible debt, and a friend paid it in full and handed you the receipt marked paid. Insisting on paying him back a little each month to "help" wouldn't be humility — it would suggest his payment wasn't enough. Works offered as payment do the same thing to the cross. "It is finished" means finished.

This also answers the lie that you must clean up your life before coming to Jesus. You don't clean up to get saved; you get saved, and God does the cleaning. Jesus doesn't say, "Come to me when you're presentable." He says, "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." Come tired. Come burdened. Come as you are. Good works still matter — but in their proper place: as gratitude, never as currency. You don't obey to become God's child; you obey because you are His child. Grace first, gratitude second. Get the order right, and you have the gospel.

Reflection Question

Are you trusting Jesus plus something — your goodness, your church record, your efforts? What would it look like today to simply receive salvation as a gift, and rest?

Closing Prayer

Father, I stop trying to earn what You have already given. I receive salvation by grace through faith alone — not because I deserve it, but because Jesus paid for it. Thank You for the gift. Teach my heart to rest in it. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 6 — Repentance and New Life

Scripture Reading

"And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel." (Mark 1:15, KJV)

"Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord." (Acts 3:19, KJV)

"Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new." (2 Corinthians 5:17, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

In the very first sermon Jesus preached, He gave the response God calls for in two words: "repent ye, and believe the gospel." Repentance and faith are two sides of one coin — and repent is one of the most misunderstood words in the Bible.

Repentance means a change of mind so deep it changes your direction. Picture yourself walking east, then turning around and walking west. That is repentance: you were walking away from God in your sin; you turn and walk toward God in faith. Feeling sorry is only emotion — Judas felt terrible and it led to despair. Repentance is the U-turn itself. And it is not a work that earns salvation; it is part of receiving the gift. Turning from sin to Christ doesn't purchase anything — it simply faces you toward the One who gives freely.

Repentance is also not perfection, and it doesn't demand that you fix everything before you come. The prodigal son didn't clean himself up in the pigpen before heading home; he just got up and went — and his father ran to meet him while he was still far off. God receives the turning heart, not the finished product. After you turn, will you still stumble? Yes — even Paul described an ongoing struggle. But your direction has changed. Before Christ, you ran toward sin. Now, when you stumble, you confess it, get up, and keep running toward God.

And here is what God does with a repentant, believing heart: He makes it new. Jesus called it being born again. Paul calls you "a new creature" — not a renovation of the old you, but a re-creation. Old things pass away; all things become new. God gives a new heart, makes you His child, and seals you with His Holy Spirit. Tomorrow, we bring it all together: how you can receive Jesus Christ — today.

Reflection Question

Is there a specific sin you've been holding onto while trying to hold onto Christ too? What would your U-turn look like — one honest step in God's direction today?

Closing Prayer

Father, I turn from my sin and I turn to Jesus. I repent, and I believe. Thank You that You receive a turning heart, not a perfect one. Make me a new creation — new heart, new direction, new life — by Your Holy Spirit. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Day 7 — Receiving Jesus Christ

Scripture Reading

"That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation." (Romans 10:9-10, KJV)

"For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved." (Romans 10:13, KJV)

"But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name." (John 1:12, KJV)

Devotional Teaching

Everything this week has been building to a personal response. You've seen God's love, your need, the Savior, His finished work, grace received by faith, and the call to repent. Now: how do you receive Him?

God's Word answers with beautiful simplicity. Believe in your heart that God raised Jesus from the dead. That is more than agreeing facts are true — even the demons believe God exists, and tremble. Heart-belief is trust: staking your life on Christ, the way a drowning man stakes everything on the rope. And confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord — saying out loud what you believe inside, and handing Him the throne of your life, not just a corner of it. Believing and confessing don't buy salvation; they are how you receive the gift.

And look at who this is for: "whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved." Not the good enough. Not the cleaned up. Whosoever. A thief on a cross next to Jesus had nothing to offer but a dying plea — and Jesus answered him, "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise." It is never too late while there's breath, and no past is too dark.

Now hear this clearly, because it matters: a prayer does not save you — Christ saves you, through repentance and faith. The prayer below is simply a way to express that faith in words, drawn from this book's own closing invitation. If you pray it, let it be the true voice of a heart that has turned from sin and is trusting Jesus alone — not a formula, but a confession. As the book itself says: the prayer itself does not save you; salvation is God's gift of grace, received through genuine faith in Jesus Christ.

A Prayer to Receive Jesus Christ

Dear Heavenly Father, I come before You today recognizing that I am a sinner and that I need Your forgiveness. I believe that Jesus Christ is Your Son, that He died on the cross for my sins, and that You raised Him from the dead. Lord Jesus, I repent of my sins and turn to You. Please forgive me, cleanse my heart, and make me new. I receive You as my Lord and Savior. Help me follow You, trust Your Word, and live according to Your will. Thank You for Your love, Your mercy, and the gift of eternal life. In Jesus' name, Amen.

If you have sincerely placed your faith in Jesus Christ, God's promise stands over you: thou shalt be saved. Begin growing in your new life: read the Bible — begin with the Gospel of John — pray honestly every day, and find a Bible-believing church where you can grow with other Christians. You have been rescued, redeemed, and restored. Eternal life starts now.

Reflection Question

Have you received Jesus Christ — turned from your sin, trusted Him in your heart, and confessed Him as Lord with your mouth? If not, what is honestly holding you back from calling on His name today?

Closing Prayer

Father in heaven, thank You for loving me and giving Your Son for me. Lord Jesus, I believe in my heart that God raised You from the dead, and I confess with my mouth: Jesus is Lord. Holy Spirit, seal me as God's own and grow new life in me. I am rescued, redeemed, and restored — by grace, through faith. In Jesus' name, Amen.
