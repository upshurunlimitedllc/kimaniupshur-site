Worry Window Scheduler

Worry feels urgent, but it rarely is. Postpone each worry to one fixed daily window, then process it there — systematically — instead of letting it run your whole day.

Educational framing: This tool uses a scheduling technique from cognitive-behavioral psychology as an educational self-reflection exercise. It is not professional mental-health advice and does not diagnose or treat anxiety. If worry is interfering with your sleep, work, or daily life, please consider talking with a licensed mental-health professional.

1. Set your daily window

Pick one 10–30 minute slot at the same time each day. Outside the window, you log worries — you don't work them.

2. Log a worry (anytime)

A worry pops up? Write it down here, then let it go until your window. One sentence is enough.

3. Process your worries (in your window)

Walk each worry through three questions, then put it on your action list or your release list.
