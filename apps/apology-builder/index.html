Apology Builder

Craft a sincere, complete apology — acknowledge what happened, own the impact, say what you'll change. A clear apology is honest communication: it doesn't guarantee how the other person will respond, and it can't replace the work of rebuilding trust over time. This is practical communication help, not counseling.

1. What happened?

Pick the situation closest to yours. Each one comes with tailored examples and "don't say this" warnings.

2. What happened — no excuses

State the specific action in plain words. No "but," no reasons, no context yet — just what you did.

3. The impact you understand

Show you get it — what did it cost them? Time, trust, a feeling, an opportunity? Don't minimize, and don't tell them how to feel.

4. What you'll change — one concrete action

Name one specific, keepable change. "I'll do better" is not a change. "Next time X happens, I will Y" is.

5. The ask

How do you want to close? A good apology expects nothing back.
