Fee Drag Detector

A 1% fee sounds small. Over 30 years, it can quietly cost you six figures. Enter your funds, your contributions, and your time horizon — this tool projects what fees will actually cost you, side by side with a low-cost alternative.

Educational modeling only — not financial advice. This is a simplified projection, not a prediction. See the assumptions at the bottom before making any investment decision.

1. Your situation

Long-run U.S. stock market average is roughly 7–10% nominal. 7% is conservative.

2. Your funds' fees

List the funds you hold (up to 3) with each fund's expense ratio — the % the fund takes every year. Pick a common example or type your own. Allocations must add up to 100%.

3. The low-cost alternative

Broad index funds commonly charge 0.03%–0.10%. Adjust if your plan's cheapest option is different.

Why this happens

An expense ratio doesn't just take money off the top — it takes money that would have compounded for decades. Every dollar paid in fees this year is a dollar that can't earn 7% next year, and the year after. That's why a "tiny" 1% fee can erase 25–30% of your ending balance over a career: fees compound against you the same way returns compound for you.
