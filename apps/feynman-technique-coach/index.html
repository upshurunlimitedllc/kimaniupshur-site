Feynman Technique Coach

Learn anything faster by explaining it simply. Write your explanation as if teaching a 12-year-old — the coach flags jargon, asks "but why?", and turns your weak spots into a study checklist.

Step 1 — Name the concept

Step 2 — Teach it back

Pretend a 12-year-old knows nothing about your concept. Explain it in your own words. Use short sentences and everyday words. No pressure — rough drafts reveal the gaps.

Step 3 — "But why?"

The coach picked the sentences that need a deeper explanation. Answer each one in plain words — or be honest and mark it a knowledge gap. Gaps are the whole point: they tell you exactly what to re-study.

Step 4 — Make an analogy

An analogy sticks when a familiar thing shares the same mechanism as the concept — not just the same vibe. Fill in both parts.

Step 5 — Rewrite it simply

Now rewrite your explanation from scratch, using what you learned: plain words, the answers to "but why?", and your analogy. Then score it.
