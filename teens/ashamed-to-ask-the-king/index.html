Ashamed to Ask the King

Ezra 8 and the Fast at the River Ahava

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

There are moments when your own words come back and stand in front of you, and you have to decide whether you actually believe them. Ezra's moment came beside a river in Babylon — with a fortune in gold on the road ahead and a king's army one request away.

Ashamed to Ask the King: Ezra 8 and the Fast at the River Ahava stays inside Ezra 8:15–31 across twelve chapters — the second return from exile, a generation after God stirred a king's heart in Ezra 1. That was God's work on a king; this is God's work on a road. Ezra had told the king, on the record, that the hand of his God is upon everyone who seeks Him for good. Now he was about to lead families, small children, and the temple treasure on a months-long road where ambush was an ordinary danger — and the king would gladly have sent soldiers. Ezra's own explanation is the book's title: “I was ashamed to require of the king a band of soldiers and horsemen to help us against the enemy in the way”. To ask would make his testimony look like a slogan. So instead of a request, he proclaimed a fast at the river Ahava, on the strength of the words he had already spoken: “The hand of our God is upon all them for good that seek him”. The book follows the gathering, the missing Levites, the embassy sent for ministers, the weighed silver and gold, and the watch kept over them — to the chapter's short, strong verdict: “So we fasted and besought our God for this: and he was intreated of us.” It is a book about integrity: living, at the level of real risk, the exact truth you have already spoken at the level of comfort. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
