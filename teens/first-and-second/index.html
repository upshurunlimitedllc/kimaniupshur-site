First and Second

Matthew 22 and the Commands That Hold the Rest

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

If someone asked you to explain your whole faith in two sentences, what would you say? Not a slogan — the real structure, the thing everything else hangs on. We collect rules, habits, opinions, church memories, and arguments, and the pile grows until nobody can say what holds it all together. A lawyer once asked Jesus a version of that question — as a trap: “Master, which is the great commandment in the law?” First and Second walks through Jesus' answer in Matthew 22 (KJV): love God with all you are — the first and great commandment — and love your neighbour as yourself, the second, like unto it. Then the sentence the whole book is built on: “On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.” The Bible is not a pile; it is a structure, and it hangs on two hooks. Chapter by chapter the book tests what the commands mean, why they come in that order, and how to hold any rule, habit, or argument up against them: does this hang on love of God, and does it hang on love of neighbour? The closing chapter ends where Jesus ended — with the Lord of the commands standing inside His own architecture, David's son and yet David's Lord, who deepened the second command on the night before He died: “That ye love one another; as I have loved you.” You are not holding up the wall by yourself. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
