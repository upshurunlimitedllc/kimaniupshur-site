Would God I Had Died for Thee

2 Samuel 18 and the Grief That Would Not Be Comforted

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

There is a sentence some teenagers need to hear from the very first page: a father's love is not cancelled by a child's rebellion.

Would God I Had Died for Thee: 2 Samuel 18 and the Grief That Would Not Be Comforted sits inside 2 Samuel 18, and inside the hard, merciful rebuke that follows in chapter 19, across twelve chapters. Before the battle, David commands his captains in front of the whole army: "Deal gently for my sake with the young man, even with Absalom". The battle is won, the rebellion is over, a throne is saved — and the king asks only one question of both messengers who reach the gate: "Is the young man Absalom safe?" Then comes the cry this book is named for: "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! would God I had died for thee, O Absalom, my son, my son!" This book refuses the cold arithmetic that says some people have forfeited the right to be grieved; it will not gloat over Absalom's death, and it will not use him to shame anyone. It follows grief that refuses to be hurried, a victory turned into mourning, and Joab's hard mercy — "speak comfortably unto thy servants" — until the king sits in the gate again, his wound unhidden, among the living. And it points where David's impossible wish pointed all along: to Jesus Christ, who actually did die for rebels, so that runaway sons and daughters can come home to the Father. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
