As Far as East from West

Psalm 103 and the Sin God Removes

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

You already did the hard part. You told the truth, named it honestly before God, and asked Him to forgive you — and Scripture says He did. So why, weeks later, does it still follow you into the room? Why does it surface on an ordinary Tuesday, in the shower, in the quiet before sleep, with the same hot wave of shame, as if nothing had ever been settled? As Far as East from West is not about how to confess — it is about what happens after. Its ground is Psalm 103:8–14 (KJV), where David stops describing his feelings and starts measuring: mercy measured by the height of heaven, removal measured by the distance from east to west, and behind the measurements a Father who pities His children because He knows exactly what they are made of. Chapter 1 begins where the forgiven teenager actually lives — confessed, and still carrying it — with the psalm's own order: “The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.” God's character is the fact; your feelings are not the meter of how He sees you. The closing chapter opens David's other psalm of forgiven sin — “Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered” — and learns to live like it is gone: not a debtor on a payment plan, but blessed. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
