Each Soul Is God’s

Ezekiel 18 and the End of the Sour-Grapes Excuse

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

"The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge." Israel's exiles quoted that proverb like a settled verdict on their lives — and God answered it with a door.

Each Soul Is God’s: Ezekiel 18 and the End of the Sour-Grapes Excuse walks through Ezekiel 18 slowly and honestly across twelve chapters, staying with the anchor spans 18:21–23 and 30–32. Teenagers hear God's answer to the proverb at full strength: "Behold, all souls are mine." They follow the chapter's generations — the just man's ordinary days, the good father's wayward son, and the son who saw his father's sins and chose otherwise — and they sit with the question the exiles thought was unanswerable: "Have I any pleasure at all that the wicked should die? saith the Lord GOD: and not that he should return from his ways, and live?" One distinction is guarded with care in chapter 3 and never blurred: harm done to a person is never the wounded one's fault, and at the same time no one's own sins are excused by inheritance. Ezekiel 36's given new heart is kept distinct from this chapter's called-for turning. The call to turn is not softened, and neither is the promise: the pattern you inherited is not the verdict on your life, and God would rather see you turn and live than anything else: "for why will ye die, O house of Israel?" Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
