The Weight Moses Carried

Numbers 11 and the God Who Shared the Burden

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

There is a kind of tired that sleep does not fix — the weight of being the one everybody looks to, the one who is supposed to hold it all together and somehow not fall apart. Moses knew that weight, and he finally told God the truth about it.

The Weight Moses Carried: Numbers 11 and the God Who Shared the Burden stays inside Numbers 11 across twelve chapters, at the speed the Bible sits with it. Teenagers hear the sound that opens the chapter — "Then Moses heard the people weep throughout their families, every man in the door of his tent" — a camp fed, free, and unsatisfied, weeping over remembered Egypt with the slavery edited out. Then they sit with Moses' own prayer, the most honest in the chapter: he is not able to bear all this people alone; it is too heavy for him; let him not see his wretchedness. God's answer is the surprise at the book's centre: He does not take the people away from Moses. He gives Moses people — seventy elders who receive of the same Spirit that is on him and carry the burden with him — Eldad and Medad included, prophesying in the camp over Joshua's protest, and Moses answering with the great-hearted wish: "would God that all the LORD's people were prophets." The load did not shrink; the shoulders multiplied. This book keeps its moment distinct from Exodus 18's delegation by counsel and structure: this is the burden shared by the Spirit, given at the very point where Moses himself was breaking — and the truth about the weight did not disqualify the carrier; it qualified him for help. For every teenager performing "I'm fine" while carrying more than one heart was built to hold, the pattern stands: pray the honest prayer, let God gather the shoulders, and refuse jealousy when the gift spreads. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
