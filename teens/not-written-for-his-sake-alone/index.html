Romans 4 and Abraham Counted Righteous

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Not Written for His Sake Alone

Romans 4 makes a promise about a sentence. When Scripture says of Abraham that he "believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness," Paul stops to insist: "Now it was not written for his sake alone, that it was imputed to him; But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead." Not Written for His Sake Alone: Romans 4 and Abraham Counted Righteous stays inside that one chapter across twelve chapters and argues it as doctrine — righteousness counted, not earned. It walks the difference between wages and a gift, hears David sing the same song in the Psalms, and shows Abraham counted righteous before any sign was given, as the father of all who believe — the man who "against hope believed in hope" and staggered not at the promise of God. Nothing here is works-earnings or self-help: the book closes where Romans 4 closes, with Christ "delivered for our offences, and raised again for our justification" — and with an invitation to give your life to Him. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
