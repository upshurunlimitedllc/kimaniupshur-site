Is Christ Divided?

1 Corinthians 1 and the Churches That Share One Lord

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Drive through almost any town and you will pass them: one church on this corner, another two streets over, a third meeting in a school hall — different names on the doors, different histories, sometimes different convictions about real questions. If there is one Jesus, why are there so many churches — and why do they argue? Is Christ Divided? stays with that question and with the first chapter of 1 Corinthians, where Paul asks it. Corinth was a gifted, energetic church — and it was splitting into fan clubs: believers lining up behind their favourite teachers and treating the lineup like loyalty to Christ Himself. Paul's answer was not a shrug. It was a question so sharp that this book takes it for its title. The book looks at the slogans the Corinthians were chanting, at why Paul found them so serious and so silly at the same time, and at the cure he kept pointing to — not a favourite teacher, not a winning argument, but one crucified Lord whom all His churches share. This is a book about the divisions themselves: how they form, what they get wrong, and how a teenager forming their first real judgments about churches and denominations can think clearly, speak fairly, and belong to Christ first. It is not a book about why you should attend church at all, and it is not mainly a book about being hurt by church, though both of those matter. It closes where Jesus' own prayer closes the subject: “That they all may be one” (John 17:21) — the church's unity as evidence offered to a watching world. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
