The Last First Day

The Corner Booth, Part Three, Book 1

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Jayden Cole's last first day of school opens with the corner booth down to two. Grace is three hours away at college, Marcus is a college freshman with a depth chart, Priya is wherever her family decided Priya would be, and Sofia is across town — so senior year at Alder Creek belongs to Jayden and Omar, and to whoever the year sends them. It sends them Danny: a sophomore who laughs half a second before his own punchlines, at his own expense, so the joke never has to stand there alone. Jayden ran that exact system for two years. This year, instead of filing it away, he stops and says hello. A shooting contest on Danny's terms — best of ten from the free-throw line, loser carries the winner's tray for a week of school days — makes them friends, and Jayden loses. Then a cafeteria mistake in front of the JV table, a joke that got away from him, nearly ends it: a week of politeness so correct it could be notarized, a confrontation, and a reconciliation in the science-wing stairwell, where Danny finally says the true thing out loud. Across the hall, Omar Haddad is fighting a quieter war — he wants one run in the drive seat at the September 16 robotics demo, and the seat has been earned by somebody else. And on a Saturday night, the Haddads' restaurant takes in a flooded-out wedding whole — forty people, a bride, and a cake in a van — because that is what the house is for. The six friends of the corner booth are scattered across three towns and two colleges — and holding. Book One of The Corner Booth: Part Three. Booth Notes: Lamentations 3:22–23.
