The Woman of Tekoah

2 Samuel 14 and the Story That Was Scripted

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

A story can fit your heart perfectly because it is true — or because it was tailored.

The Woman of Tekoah: 2 Samuel 14 and the Story That Was Scripted stays on its text across twelve chapters, and it does not hide the machinery: Joab perceives that the king's heart is toward his banished son, fetches a wise woman from Tekoah, dresses her in mourning worn on purpose, and puts the words in her mouth. Teenagers watch the whole production built plank by plank — a perceived heart, a cast messenger, a costume, a script — and then watch it performed: a widow's plea, two sons in a field with none to part them, and one coal about to be quenched, until the king has sworn an oath on a stranger's case and the woman reads his own ruling back to him: the king "doth not fetch home again his banished". This is a discernment book, not a media manual — the modern campaigns, clips, and causes it touches are servants of the chapter's lesson, never replacements for it — and it treats the woman with the respect the text itself gives her: Scripture calls her wise, and her scripted case carried a true application, grounded in a true sentence about God, who "doth he devise means, that his banished be not expelled from him". The goal is not suspicion of every story. The goal is a teenager who can feel a story fully — and still ask David's question: "Is not the hand of Joab with thee in all this?" For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
