Much Fruit from One Grain

John 12 and the Death That Multiplies

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Some Greeks came to Jerusalem with a simple request: “We would see Jesus.” The answer they received was not an introduction. It was a grain of wheat, a death, and the mathematics of multiplication.

Much Fruit from One Grain: John 12 and the Death That Multiplies stays inside John 12 across twelve chapters, built on Jesus' own words in verses 24–26 with the chapter around them held at frame weight. The saying stands at the book's centre, given in full: “Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit.” The book refuses to soften the paradox that follows: “He that loveth his life shall lose it; and he that hateth his life in this world shall keep it unto life eternal.” It follows the call to serve and follow — and the Father's honour promised to the one who does — through the troubled soul of Jesus, the voice from heaven that came for the hearers' sakes, and the chapter's horizon: “And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.” The anointing at Bethany earlier in the chapter is not retold here, and no other Gospel scene is imported; the grain saying carries the book, exactly as the chapter carries it — as the explanation Jesus gave of His own approaching death, and the pattern He set before anyone who would follow Him. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
