Jeremiah 28 and the Prophet Who Broke the Yoke

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

There is a kind of lie that does not feel like a lie. It feels like relief. Yokes of Iron: Jeremiah 28 and the Prophet Who Broke the Yoke tells the story of Hananiah, who stood in the house of God and promised everything Judah wanted — the vessels home, the king home, the captives home, "within two full years" — and broke the wooden yoke off Jeremiah's neck to prove it. Jeremiah's first answer was "Amen." His second was the test Scripture itself gives: the prophet of peace is known when his word comes to pass. Then the word of the LORD came, and the answer to a broken wooden yoke was not a softer one: "thou shalt make for them yokes of iron" — and to the prophet who had made the people trust a lie: "The LORD hath not sent thee". This book names the comfortable-lie market for what it is and teaches the reader to tell a true word from a welcome one. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
