Never Enough

Perfectionism and the Pressure to Be Perfect

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Never Enough — subtitled Perfectionism and the Pressure to Be Perfect — follows Zoe, a seventeen-year-old with a 4.3 GPA, three AP classes, varsity soccer and student council, who still hears one voice after every win: never enough, and it answers that voice with the book's triad, Rest. Grace. Free. Across twelve chapters in three movements — The Treadmill, The Gift, and The Way Off — it names the treadmill as envy, vapor and anxious toil, then opens the gift of grace that is not earned, no condemnation, Sabbath rest and work done as worship instead of proof, before walking the way off through falling and rising, strength in weakness, two minutes of stillness, and going forward, not perfect; every chapter ends with one 'Your move this week' practice, and a note up front urges any reader whose pressure has gone deeper to tell a trusted adult today. Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
