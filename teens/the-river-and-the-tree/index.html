The River and the Tree

Revelation 22 and the Last Page of the Bible

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Most last pages close a world. The Bible's last page opens one — a river, a tree, a face, and an invitation that is still open.

The River and the Tree: Revelation 22 and the Last Page of the Bible stays inside Revelation 22:1–5 across twelve chapters, walking the Bible's final description of how everything ends slowly, one picture at a time, in John's own order: "a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb"; the tree of life on either side of the river, bearing twelve manner of fruits every month, its leaves "for the healing of the nations"; no more curse; servants who see His face, bear His name, and reign for ever and ever in a city with no night there. Two honest boundaries govern the telling. The book does not speculate beyond John's words — no timelines, no charts, no guesses about details God has not revealed. And it keeps John's picture distinct from every earlier picture that resembles it: Isaiah's is prophetic imagery in which death is still named, Ezekiel's river is a measured one, and neither is borrowed to fill Revelation 22, which is the final eternal state itself. The Bible ends where it began — in a garden, beside a river, under a tree — except that everything that went wrong in the first garden is, on the last page, finally and forever put right. And the page finishes as an invitation, spoken in a chorus that recruits its hearers as callers: "And the Spirit and the bride say, Come" — "whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely." Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
