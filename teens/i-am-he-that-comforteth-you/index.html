I Am He That Comforteth You

Isaiah 51 and the Fear of Man Answered

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Comfort usually arrives in your life as a feeling you are told to produce — cheer up, calm down, do not worry about them.

Comfort usually arrives in your life as a feeling you are told to produce — cheer up, calm down, do not worry about them. Isaiah 51 does something entirely different, and I Am He That Comforteth You: Isaiah 51 and the Fear of Man Answered walks through Isaiah 51:12–16 in order, across twelve chapters. Before God tells His people anything about their fear, He tells them about Himself: "I, even I, am he that comforteth you" — heaven speaks first, and only then is the feared thing measured: "who art thou, that thou shouldest be afraid of a man that shall die, and of the son of man which shall be made as grass". Fear of people is treated in this passage as a memory failure — forgetting the LORD your Maker, who stretched forth the heavens and laid the foundations of the earth — and its cure is not a braver performance but a recovered memory: the captive exile hastens to be loosed, the fury of the oppressor is asked after and found empty, God's words are put in His people's mouths, and the strongest hand in existence does its gentlest work: "I have covered thee in the shadow of mine hand". A shadow does not remove you from the corridor or the audience; it keeps you from being scorched in them. And the passage ends with the only verdict with the authority to settle identity — God says to Zion, "Thou art my people" — not the crowd's caption, not the oppressor's, and not your own worst self-assessment on a fear-filled night. This book will not ask you, first, to be braver. It will ask you to be reminded. For teenagers ages 13-18.
