Woe Is Me

Isaiah 6 and Undone Before a Holy God

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

There is a kind of honesty that only shows up when you stop performing. Most of us spend our days managing an image — at school, online, even at church — adjusting what people see so that we look steadier, cleaner, and more together than we feel. And then, once in a while, something breaks through the management, and the honest sentence that rises up is not flattering at all. Isaiah had that moment in a temple, in front of a throne. He was not a beginner and he was not a rebel. He was a prophet — a man whose whole job was speaking for God — and when he saw the King in His holiness, the first true words out of his mouth were a confession: “Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips” (Isaiah 6:5). Woe Is Me stays inside that moment, in the first seven verses of Isaiah 6, and walks slowly through what happened: the vision — the throne high and lifted up, standing empty and blazing, its train filling the temple, the seraphim with covered faces — the undoing, and the coal from the altar that cleansed him: “thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged” (Isaiah 6:7). Before God sent Isaiah anywhere, God undid him — and then made him clean. That order matters: the cleansed are not on probation, and undone and clean are carried together, in that order, for the rest of a life. This is good news for anyone who has ever seen themselves clearly and wondered if they were finished. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
