Only upon God

Psalm 62 and the Soul That Waits for God Alone

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Every day your expectation runs somewhere first — to the group chat, to the result you are waiting on, to the plan you can control tightly enough that nothing surprises you. Psalm 62 is a soul deliberately sending its expectation to one address, and learning to be quiet there.

Only upon God: Psalm 62 and the Soul That Waits for God Alone walks through Psalm 62 verse by verse across twelve chapters, in the psalm's own order — the resting psalm, not a shelter-in-danger telling. Teenagers begin with David's confession, stated before any request is made (in this psalm, remarkably, none ever is): "Truly my soul waiteth upon God: from him cometh my salvation." Then they follow the word that beats through every verse: only. "He only is my rock." The psalm weighs its rival resting places on an honest scale — men of low degree and men of high degree together "lighter than vanity," riches that increase and must not have the heart set upon them — and teaches the practices that hold expectation in its one place: "pour out your heart before him," trust in Him at all times, and the settled verdicts the soul talks back to itself with: "power belongeth unto God," and "unto thee, O Lord, belongeth mercy." The neighbouring psalms of danger are kept as stated contrasts only; this book stays with the soul that waits, and with David's own self-instruction — "my soul, wait thou only upon God" — until the word only sounds less like a restriction and more like relief. David ends the psalm still waiting, and so does the book: not a technique mastered, but an address memorized. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
