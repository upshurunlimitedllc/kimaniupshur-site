Made Like unto His Brethren

Hebrews 2 and the Captain Who Suffered

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Hebrews 2 does not begin by telling you that Jesus understands you. It proves why the comfort is real — step by step, the way a careful builder lays a foundation before anyone talks about the house.

Made Like unto His Brethren: Hebrews 2 and the Captain Who Suffered stays inside Hebrews 2 across twelve chapters, in the chapter's own order. It opens with the warning: we ought to give "the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard," lest at any time we "should let them slip" — drift, not rebellion, is the first danger named. Then the world to come, and man's astonishing place in it; then the sight the whole book leans on: "But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour." Teenagers follow the Captain of their salvation, made perfect through sufferings, who "is not ashamed to call them brethren" — who took real flesh and blood, "he also himself likewise took part of the same," broke through death the power of the one who held it, and freed people who had lived their whole lives in bondage to fear. The book lands on the verses it is named for: made like unto His brethren in all things, He is a merciful and faithful high priest — "For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted." Nothing was held back, nothing was faked, nothing was skipped: that completeness is the book's claim. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
