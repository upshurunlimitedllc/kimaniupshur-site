Not Broken, Not Quenched

Isaiah 42 and the Servant Who Does Not Cry Out

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

The Servant of Isaiah 42 does not arrive shouting. He does not break the bruised reed or snuff out the smoking flax — and it is precisely this Servant, gentle with everything fragile, who is given the nations for His work.

Not Broken, Not Quenched: Isaiah 42 and the Servant Who Does Not Cry Out stays inside Isaiah 42 across twelve chapters, following the first Servant song in its own order. God presents Him in His own words: “Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth”. Then comes the manner of His work — He shall not cry, nor lift up, nor cause His voice to be heard in the street — and the promise this book is named for: “A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench: he shall bring forth judgment unto truth.” The book follows the song outward: the LORD who created the heavens and gives breath to the people upon the earth calls His Servant in righteousness, holds His hand, and gives Him for a covenant and a light to the Gentiles — “To open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison”. And it keeps the song's guard-rail in view: “I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another”. The later Servant songs are pointed to as a sequence, never plundered — Isaiah 53's wounding and Isaiah 61's proclamation belong to their own books. This one stays with the Servant who does not fail nor is discouraged, and with what His gentleness means for everyone who has ever felt like the reed or the flax. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
