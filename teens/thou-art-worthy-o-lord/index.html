Thou Art Worthy, O Lord

Revelation 4 and the Throne Before the Scroll

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Before anything in Revelation breaks open — before any seal, trumpet, or judgment — God shows John a room. Thou Art Worthy, O Lord: Revelation 4 and the Throne Before the Scroll stays in that one room for twelve chapters and does not leave it. It begins with a door opened in heaven and a voice as of a trumpet saying "Come up hither" — an invitation John did not force, earn, or even ask for, given to a man exiled on an island for his faith. Inside the door, the first sight is the centre of everything: "a throne was set in heaven, and one sat on the throne" — set before John arrived, occupied before anything else is allowed to happen, and not under threat of removal. Chapter by chapter the book walks the room slowly: the light like a jasper and a sardine stone, the single emerald bow round about the throne, the seven lamps of fire burning before the throne, the sea of glass like unto crystal, the four living creatures who rest not day and night, saying, "Holy, holy, holy", and the four and twenty elders who fall down before Him and cast their crowns before the throne. Everything lands on the chapter's own verdict: "Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created." Worthiness in Revelation 4 is grounded in creation, not in power alone — which means a teenager's worth, days, and future rest on the Creator who made them on purpose, not on how impressive they become. There is no scroll in these pages, and no seals are opened here; the scroll belongs to the next chapter and another book. What this book teaches first is the order God Himself chose — the throne before the crisis — so that everything read afterward, in Revelation and in life, is read with a settled heart, knowing in front of Whom it all happens. For teenagers ages 13-18.
