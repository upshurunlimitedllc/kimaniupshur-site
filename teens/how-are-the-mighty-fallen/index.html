How Are the Mighty Fallen

2 Samuel 1 and David's Lament for His Enemy

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

The messenger who brought David the news of Saul's death expected a reward. He had edited himself into the centre of the story — and he had misread the king completely.

How Are the Mighty Fallen: 2 Samuel 1 and David's Lament for His Enemy stays inside 2 Samuel 1 across twelve chapters: two days in Ziklag, then a runner from the battle with his clothes rent and earth upon his head, carrying a crown and a bracelet and a version of Saul's death with himself cast as its hero. Teenagers watch David cross-examine the story — how went the matter, whence art thou — and then watch the court's expectation break: the king and his men mourned, and wept, and fasted for the enemy who had hunted David for years, and the messenger's edited story earned him the sentence he never imagined. Only then does the book reach the lament itself, the song David ordered taught to the children of Judah. It begins with a refusal: “Tell it not in Gath, publish it not in the streets of Askelon” — grief will not be handed over as the enemy's entertainment. It honours Saul and Jonathan together, the king who fell and the friend who loved David, whose love David calls “passing the love of women” — covenant friendship in the text's own terms: chosen, sworn, loyal at cost. And over the whole song beats the refrain that gives the book its name: “How are the mighty fallen in the midst of the battle!” until the last line lands it: “How are the mighty fallen, and the weapons of war perished!” There is no gloating anywhere in this book, because there is none in David's song — this is what it looks like to grieve a fall you did not cause, of a man who wronged you, without rewriting either the wrong or the grief. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
