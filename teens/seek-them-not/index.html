Seek Them Not

Jeremiah 45 and the Small Promise to Baruch

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Some teenagers need to hear that God addresses a whole chapter of Scripture to the person holding the pen.

Seek Them Not: Jeremiah 45 and the Small Promise to Baruch walks slowly through the shortest chapter in Jeremiah — five verses spoken to one tired scribe — across twelve chapters. Baruch has written out the scroll of his own nation's judgment at Jeremiah's dictation, and he has worn down to a single sentence of grief: "Woe is me now! for the LORD hath added grief to my sorrow; I fainted in my sighing, and I find no rest." God quotes the lament back to him, word for word, before He answers it — proof that the sighing was heard, stored, and taken seriously. Then comes the truth about the times at full scale — what God has built He will break down, even this whole land — and then the searching audit of ambition: "And seekest thou great things for thyself? seek them not". This book will not condemn a worn-down reader: Baruch is answered, never rebuked for his exhaustion, and ambition is audited here, not mocked — renouncing great things is no technique for becoming great. What God gives instead is smaller on the surface and greater in fact: "but thy life will I give unto thee for a prey in all places whither thou goest" — life itself, snatched like spoil out of a falling world, given by grace, and portable to every road a life can take. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
