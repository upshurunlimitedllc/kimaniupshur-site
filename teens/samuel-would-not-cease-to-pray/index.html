Samuel Would Not Cease to Pray

1 Samuel 12 and the Farewell of the Last Judge

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

He had led them since childhood, and they had just replaced him. Everything in human experience says this is the moment a set-aside leader goes quiet, goes home, and lets the people discover the hard way. Samuel made a promise instead — and called breaking it a sin against God.

Samuel Would Not Cease to Pray: 1 Samuel 12 and the Farewell of the Last Judge stays inside 1 Samuel 12 across twelve chapters — a whole education in how to love people who have not listened to you. Israel has asked for a king, received a king, and gathered at Gilgal, and Samuel, old and greyheaded, gives the era's closing address. He audits his own life in public — “whose ox have I taken?” — and lets the people acquit him. He retells their whole history, not to shame them but to remind them whose hand had carried them. He calls down thunder and rain in the middle of wheat harvest so they can feel in their skin how serious their choice has been. And then, when they are frightened and begging him to pray for thy servants, this rejected old man makes one of the most astonishing promises in the Bible: “God forbid that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you: but I will teach you the good and the right way.” Read the direction of the sin carefully: if Samuel stopped praying, the sin would be against the LORD, not merely against Israel — they never hired him to pray; God did — so their rejection cannot release him from it. The farewell ends with memory as its final gift: “consider how great things he hath done for you.” If you have ever given good advice and watched it ignored, warned someone you love and been set aside, or wondered whether prayer for such people is worth anything at all, Samuel's farewell was preserved for you. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
