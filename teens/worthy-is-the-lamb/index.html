Worthy Is the Lamb

Revelation 5 and the Scroll Only One Could Open

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

A sealed book rests in the right hand of God, and a strong angel's question goes out through heaven and earth and under the earth — and no one, anywhere, can answer it.

Worthy Is the Lamb: Revelation 5 and the Scroll Only One Could Open stays inside Revelation 5 across twelve chapters, and begins with the question itself: “Who is worthy to open the book, and to loose the seals thereof?” Teenagers sit with John in the silence that follows — no man in heaven, nor in earth, neither under the earth — and with his response, which this book treats as a lesson in what our tears reveal about what we value: “And I wept much, because no man was found worthy to open and to read the book, neither to look thereon.” Then the correction, spoken by an elder: “Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof.” A Lion is announced — and a Lamb is seen, standing as it had been slain, in a chapter this book reads with Revelation's own restraint: the One on the throne is never described, and only the Lamb is pictured. He came and took the book, and heaven answered with harps and golden vials and a new song, then with ten thousand times ten thousand voices, until the verdict this book is named for filled everything: “Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing.” Every creature joins it, and the last word is Amen. For teenagers holding sealed chapters of their own — futures they cannot read, questions God has not answered — this book locates them: in the right hand of God, and openable by the pierced hands alone. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
