Minister Grace

Ephesians 4 and Speech That Builds Instead of Rotting

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

You produce more speech before breakfast than your grandparents produced in a week — and now every word of it stays.

Minister Grace: Ephesians 4 and Speech That Builds Instead of Rotting is built on one verse — Ephesians 4:29 — and on the single question it asks of every word, across twelve chapters: "Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers." Teenagers learn Paul's two-way test with no third option: corrupt is a rot word, for speech that decays a hearer from the inside while looking fine on the surface; edifying is a building word, for speech that leaves a person steadier and more able to stand. There is no neutral pile — not for the midnight comment, the captioned screenshot, the "joking" message in the group chat, or the compliment with one small hook in it. The neighbouring questions are named and left to their own books — whether a thing is true, whether to believe what you hear, whether you have heard the whole story, whether a guard stands at your mouth. This book asks the purpose question last and on purpose: your words are true, believed carefully, judged fairly, and guarded — but what are they for? Rightly aimed, they do the startling thing Paul promises: they minister grace to the hearers. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
