The God of Hope

Romans 15 and Joy and Peace in Believing

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

After fourteen chapters of argument, Paul stops arguing — and prays one sentence over a divided church: that the God of hope would fill His people with joy and peace until hope itself overflows.

The God of Hope: Romans 15 and Joy and Peace in Believing stays inside Romans 15 across twelve chapters, walking toward the blessing the book is named for. Teenagers start where the chapter starts: "We then that are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves" — Christ, who pleased not Himself, as the pattern. They sit under the chapter's promise about the past: "For whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning" — the Scriptures given so that patience and comfort would end in hope. They watch Jew and Gentile received into one choir — one mind, one mouth — through four Old Testament quotations saying the same astonishing thing, until the song reaches the nations: "Praise the Lord, all ye Gentiles; and laud him, all ye people." Only then does the blessing land, in its own words: "Now the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that ye may abound in hope, through the power of the Holy Ghost." Hope in this chapter is not a mood to hunt down on a good day. It has a source, and the source is a Person; it has a means, and the means is believing; and it has an overflow, because a God who fills does not stop at the rim. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
