Graven on His Palms

Isaiah 49 and the God Who Cannot Forget

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

This book is about a quiet fear: not that God is angry — that He has forgotten you.

Graven on His Palms: Isaiah 49 and the God Who Cannot Forget begins where God's own people began, with the sentence Zion said out loud: "The LORD hath forsaken me, and my Lord hath forgotten me." God does not scold her for saying it. He answers it — and His answer is the strongest picture of being remembered in the whole Bible. Across twelve chapters, teenagers hear it at full strength: a mother may forget the child at her breast — "yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee" — and then the step beyond even that: "Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me." The book keeps the picture the way God gave it, as a figure of speech and never a claim about anatomy, and follows what it means: a God who has called you by your name, who hears in an acceptable time, and whose people shall not be ashamed that wait for Him. If you have ever prayed and felt the prayer go nowhere, this is Scripture answering that exact fear. Every chapter ends with one step to take — a Do, a Reflect, or a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a personal invitation to give your life to Jesus Christ. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
