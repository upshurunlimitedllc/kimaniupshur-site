Go to Him First

Matthew 18:15 and the Conversation Before the Fallout

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Teenagers • Ages 13–18

Somebody has hurt you, or wronged you, or done the thing you cannot stop replaying — and now there is a moment, a small, ordinary, decisive moment, where you choose who hears about it first. The fastest choice is almost never the private one. It is a screenshot. A group chat. A post vague enough that everyone knows exactly who it is about. Within an hour, a fault that belonged to two people belongs to twenty, and the one person who could actually do something about it is the last one to hear it, and hears it from everybody else. Jesus gave a different order. Go to Him First is built on Matthew 18:15 — go to the person, tell him his fault between the two of you alone, first, before the fallout — and on the goal Jesus sets inside it: not winning, but gaining. “Thou hast gained thy brother.” A brother gained is worth more than an argument won, every time. One honest note stands at the front of the book, before Chapter 1: a named standalone section, “A Safeguarding Exception: When You Must Not Go Alone,” placed in the table of contents where nobody can miss it — Matthew 18's private conversation does NOT apply to abuse, threats, exploitation, or danger; there, the teenager goes FIRST to trusted adults and proper authorities, and that is obedience to the higher duty of safety, not a failure of the pattern. Chapter 8 (“When Private Is Not Safe”) reinforces it. And the book says openly what its closing chapter does: in Matthew 18, restoration comes before forgiveness — the chapter's second half, seventy times seven, is walked into on purpose in Chapter 12, with the parable's full telling commended to its companion book, The Hardest Forgiveness, without retelling it here. For teenagers ages 13-18.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
