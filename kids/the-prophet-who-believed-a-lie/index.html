The Prophet Who Believed a Lie

1 Kings 13 and the Man of God Who Stopped Checking

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

The man of God had done everything right — cried against the altar, refused the king's reward, started home by the road God gave him. Then an older prophet rode after him with a story he could not check, and he believed it.

The Prophet Who Believed a Lie: 1 Kings 13 and the Man of God Who Stopped Checking stays inside 1 Kings 13 across ten chapters, with its anchor at verses 11–24. Children watch the unnamed man of God from Judah cry "O altar, altar, thus saith the LORD" at Bethel, refuse half of Jeroboam's house, and set out hungry on the other road home — then meet the old prophet of Bethel, the rest under the oak, the meal at the table, and the three flat words at the story's centre: "But he lied unto him." The lie is named as a lie, plainly and repeatedly, exactly as the chapter names it; the judgment on the road is told at the text's own weight, without gore; and the old prophet's grief — "Alas, my brother!" — is left standing without excusing anything, even as he ends by trusting the true word he once worked against, the word that "shall surely come to pass." Three men, three warnings: a king who saw everything and changed nothing, a prophet who lied, and a man of God who stopped checking. The book closes by teaching the check itself, in four questions for any spiritual claim, whoever is speaking: what has God already said? does the new claim match it, or cancel it? can the claim be checked? and who benefits if I stop checking? Age is not verification. A title is not verification. Confidence is not verification. God's Word, checked carefully, is. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
