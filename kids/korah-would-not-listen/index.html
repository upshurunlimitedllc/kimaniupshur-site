Korah Would Not Listen

Numbers 16 and the Rebellion That Shook the Camp

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

Aaron stood "between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed." Before that run with the censer, there was a rebellion — begun by a man who already served close to God's tent.

Korah Would Not Listen: Numbers 16 and the Rebellion That Shook the Camp stays inside Numbers 16:1–35 and 46–48, told truthfully at the text's own weight across ten chapters. Korah was a Kohathite Levite who carried the holiest furniture of the tabernacle on his shoulders — but a good job was not enough, and his envy dressed itself as equality: "all the congregation are holy." Children watch Moses fall on his face and refuse to defend his own honour — "the man whom the LORD doth choose, he shall be holy" — the camp move away from the rebels' tents, the earth open, and then the book's centre: Aaron running with his censer into the plague to stand between the dead and the living. The judgment is stated plainly, as Scripture states it, with nothing graphic described or shown, and the emphasis rests where the chapter rests it — on intercession. Verses 36–45 are passed over in summary, and the Note to Grown-Ups says so. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray, and the Note asks grown-ups not to skip them. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
