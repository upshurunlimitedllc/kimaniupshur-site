Ask Before You Share

The Golden Rule for Cameras and Phones

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

Before you take the picture, ask. Ask Before You Share is built on Matthew 7, and above all on its Golden Rule, quoted exactly: “Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets” (Matthew 7:12). From that one verse the book teaches consent at three separate moments — three gates. Chapter 3: ask before you PHOTOGRAPH someone. Chapter 4: ask before you RECORD them, in video or voice — a recording needs its own separate consent. Chapter 5: ask before you POST or SHARE an image of them — because consent can be specific (yes to a family album can be no to a public page), and a changed mind is honoured. Then the privacy lessons a child with a phone actually needs: private chats stay private, and a screenshot IS a share (Chapter 6); once posted, an image cannot be fully taken back (Chapter 7); a child's own “no” about their image is valid (Chapter 8); and Chapter 9 gives the safeguarding rule plainly — if anyone, of any age, asks a child to send images of themselves, the child does not send them and tells a trusted grown-up straight away: no secret about pictures is ever a secret you have to keep. The scenarios stay kind throughout — a frosting fleck on a nose, a laughing trip over a football — nobody is shamed or mocked, and the lesson lands through the asking. In every picture, every screen shows an abstract glow only: no app, no message, no readable word. Every quotation is a verbatim part of one single verse of Matthew 7 in the King James Version, never verses joined together. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray: one simple action, and one short prayer in quotes to read together. For children ages 6-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
