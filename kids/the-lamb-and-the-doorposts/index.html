The Lamb and the Doorposts

Exodus 12 and the Night God Passed Over

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

One chapter of the Bible, one night — the night of the first Passover. The Lamb and the Doorposts stays inside Exodus 12 from beginning to end: the lamb chosen for each house and kept without blemish, the blood struck with hyssop on the two side posts and the upper door post, the meal of roast lamb, unleavened bread, and bitter herbs eaten with loins girded, shoes on, and staff in hand, ready to travel. At Grandpa Samuel's kitchen table, Zara and Eli walk through the chapter verse by verse on the big worn Bible, hearing God's own words quoted word-for-word from the King James Version — including His promise, “when I see the blood, I will pass over you.” God's judgment on Egypt is taught plainly and soberly, the way the chapter tells it: Exodus 12:12 is quoted in full, the tenth plague is named as real, and nothing is hidden or softened into a metaphor — and nothing is sensationalized, in the words or in the pictures. The book stays inside Exodus 12 throughout; the departure from Egypt and the Red Sea belong to companion tellings. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray — one simple action, and one short prayer in quotes to read together. For children ages 6-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
