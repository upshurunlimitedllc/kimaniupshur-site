The Child in the Middle

Matthew 18 and the Greatness of Small

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

In a house in Capernaum, the disciples came to Jesus with a very grown-up question: “Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?” Jesus did not answer with a list or a lecture. He called a little child, set him in the midst of them, and let the child be the lesson. Zara hears the story first on her grandmother's porch — she had spent that very morning whispering “Please let me be first” at a school race, and her little brother Theo had argued over the bigger half of a peach, so the disciples' question sounds familiar from the inside. The Child in the Middle stays with Matthew 18:1–5 (KJV), and with that passage alone, told for younger children: the call to be converted and become as little children; the answer that whoever humbles himself as this little child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven; and the promise that whoever receives one such little child in Jesus' name receives Jesus Himself. This is the Matthew 18 lesson — a child made the sermon for grown men's ambition — and it is kept distinct from the blessing scene of Matthew 19 and Mark 10, which is never staged here. The closing chapter returns to that room in Capernaum one last time: someone your size, at the centre of the lesson — greatness that keeps a trusting, humble, welcoming heart at every size. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray — one simple action, and one short prayer to read together. For children ages 6-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
