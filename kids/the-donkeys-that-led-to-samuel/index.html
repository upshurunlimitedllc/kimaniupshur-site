The Donkeys That Led to Samuel

1 Samuel 9 and the Errand That Was Bigger Than It Looked

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

The donkeys were lost — and that small, ordinary, annoying errand was quietly carrying Saul to an anointing he knew nothing about.

The Donkeys That Led to Samuel: 1 Samuel 9 and the Errand That Was Bigger Than It Looked retells 1 Samuel 9:3–10, 15–27 and 10:1 across ten read-aloud chapters. Children meet Kish's tall son Saul — "from his shoulders and upward he was higher than any of the people" — sent out with a servant on the plainest errand in Israel: "Take now one of the servants with thee, and arise, go seek the asses." They follow the fruitless search town after town, Saul's sensible wish to turn back, and the servant's good idea: find the man of God, whose word always comes to pass. They hear what Saul could not know — that a day before he came, the LORD had told Samuel in his ear the very hour of his arrival — and they watch the welcome unfold: "I am the seer," a seat kept at the feast, a portion kept for Saul, and Samuel's word on the rooftop at dawn: "stand thou still a while, that I may shew thee the word of God." The book stops where its passage stops, at the anointing itself: "the LORD hath anointed thee to be captain over his inheritance." Saul's later failure is not told here. What children carry away is the book's quiet claim: God guides His people through ordinary errands, closed doors, wise companions, and moments of standing still — and no day walked faithfully with Him is ever only what it looks like. Every chapter ends with a simple Do and a short Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For younger children ages 6-8.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
