The Cave Where David Said No

1 Samuel 24 and Mercy for a King Who Hunted Him

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

David had done nothing wrong, and still he had to hide. The Cave Where David Said No tells 1 Samuel 24 whole, and that chapter alone: Saul returned from the Philistines, three thousand chosen men, the rocks of the wild goats at En-gedi — and the cave where the king came in alone while David and his men remained in its sides. There, David's men whispered that this was the day the LORD had spoken of. David crept forward and cut off only the skirt of Saul's robe — and even that smote his heart: “The LORD forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the LORD's anointed.” The book's named handling is restraint as strength, and it teaches one distinction carefully: the men's words in verse 4 were the men's claim about what God had said, never a recorded word of God. Outside the cave, David calls “My lord the king,” bows, and holds up the skirt as proof of a life spared, leaving the judgment to the LORD — “The LORD judge between me and thee” — and Saul weeps and answers, “Thou art more righteous than I.” The knife appears only against cloth; no harm to any person is depicted or celebrated. Every quotation is a verbatim part of one single verse of 1 Samuel 24 in the King James Version, never verses joined together. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray. For children ages 6-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
