Imagine coming home hungry at the end of a long day and finding the house already built, the door already open, and everything prepared before you even knew you were hungry. Wisdom Hath Builded Her House: Proverbs 9 and the Feast on the Highest Place tours Proverbs 9:1–6 room by room, across ten chapters. "Wisdom hath builded her house, she hath hewn out her seven pillars:" — the building came first, before a single guest had knocked; Wisdom did not ask the guests to bring bricks or prove they deserved a seat. The table is furnished, the maidens are sent, and the cry goes up from the highest places of the city: "Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither:" — and to the one who wants understanding, "Come, eat of my bread, and drink of the wine which I have mingled." The guests are not the clever being rewarded; they are the simple being honoured, and the feast is invitation and gift, never wages. Nor do the guests stay seated: "Forsake the foolish, and live; and go in the way of understanding." Folly's rival house is named in prose only — never quoted, developed, or dramatized — and the whole picture finally points past itself to Jesus Christ, the wisdom of God in person, who called, fed, welcomed the small, and gave Himself so sinners could be brought to God's table forever. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
