"And the word of the LORD came unto me, saying," — so begins one of the most searching chapters in the Bible, and I Myself Will Search: Ezekiel 34 and the Shepherd Who Comes Himself walks through Ezekiel 34 in order, across ten chapters. The shepherds of Israel fed themselves instead of the flock: the diseased were not strengthened, the sick not healed, the broken not bound up, the driven away not brought back, the lost not sought — and the sheep were scattered over every mountain, with none searching or seeking after them. Then the Owner of the flock speaks for Himself: "Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out." God promises to perform every verb the shepherds refused — to seek the lost and deliver them out of all places, even in the cloudy and dark day, to gather them and feed them in a good pasture, and to "bind up that which was broken, and will strengthen that which was sick" — until the flock knows that He is with them and that they are His people. The book carries the chapter at its full weight and holds its fences: God's anger falls on shepherds who chose selfishness, never on the sheep who suffered under them and never on a struggling child; and the searching is all gift — God's own "I will", never wages the flock earns. The Good Shepherd of John 10 is pointed to in prose as the Father's ancient promise kept. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
