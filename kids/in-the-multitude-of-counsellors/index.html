In the Multitude of Counsellors

Proverbs 15 and Plans That Do Not Fall

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

A bridge that falls down on paper teaches a better lesson than one that falls down in real life — and Proverbs says the difference is made before the building starts, by the people you ask.

In the Multitude of Counsellors: Proverbs 15 and Plans That Do Not Fall opens with a model bridge that collapsed on the drawing board, and builds ten chapters on the anchor sentence of Proverbs 15:22: “Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.” Children learn what counsel actually is — not a committee, not a vote, and not asking until somebody agrees with you — and who counts as a counsellor: the grandmother, the teacher, the grandfather, the people God has placed close enough to see what you cannot. The supporting sentences of Proverbs stand in their own words: “The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise.” The book teaches the timing of asking — before the plan hardens, while it can still be changed — and what to do when counsellors disagree, because disagreement handled well is part of how a plan gets strong. Hard conversations and big journeys get their own chapter: the bigger the plan, the more foolish it is to carry it alone. (King Rehoboam's famous refusal of counsel in 1 Kings 12 is named only to be fenced off — the principle here is carried by Proverbs itself and by modern planning scenes a child will recognise.) Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
