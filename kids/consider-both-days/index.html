Consider Both Days

Ecclesiastes 7 and Wisdom for Prosperity and Adversity

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

“In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider.” One week held both kinds of day — and a wise old book taught Maya how to think inside each one.

Consider Both Days: Ecclesiastes 7 and Wisdom for Prosperity and Adversity is built entirely on Ecclesiastes 7:8–14, with supporting verses from the same chapter, across ten read-aloud chapters. Through Maya, her little brother Caleb, and Grandma Rose’s big Bible on the porch, children learn the thinking skill the Preacher teaches: considering — holding two true things at once, and refusing both the proud conclusion of a good day and the despairing conclusion of a hard one. They meet the chapter’s counsel in its order: “Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof”; patience better than pride of spirit; anger that “resteth in the bosom of fools”; the trap of asking why the former days were better than these; and wisdom as a defence that the weather cannot reach. The balance of verse 14 is kept in full — “God also hath set the one over against the other” — so the hard days in these stories (a rained-out picnic, a lost match, a friend’s family moving away) are never flattened into mere setups for happy endings, and the crooked things of verse 13 stay crooked: “who can make that straight, which he hath made crooked?” The book deliberately ends in consideration rather than in either triumph or despair. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
