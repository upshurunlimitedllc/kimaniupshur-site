They Told the Whole Story Back to God

Nehemiah 9 and the Long Prayer of a People Remembering

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

The feast was over, and the people stood up — hungry, dusty, and honest — to do something nobody had taught them to perform: tell God His own story, all of it, in order.

They Told the Whole Story Back to God: Nehemiah 9 and the Long Prayer of a People Remembering stays inside Nehemiah 9 across ten chapters — the day after the Feast of Booths, when the returned people of Jerusalem fasted in sackcloth, confessed their own sins and the sins of their fathers, and heard the day divide itself in two: "they confessed, and worshipped the LORD their God." Children follow the Levites' great prayer in its own order, from the call — "Stand up and bless the LORD your God for ever and ever" — through creation ("Thou, even thou, art LORD alone; thou hast made heaven"), the choosing of Abraham, the cry heard by the Red Sea, the pillars of cloud and fire, and the bread from heaven. Then the prayer turns, and does not blink: hardened necks, a golden calf — and a God who is "ready to pardon, gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness," who forsook His people not. The wheel of the judges' years turns, exile comes and does not have the last word, and the prayer lands where honest prayer lands: "thou hast done right, but we have done wickedly" — and a covenant written and sealed. Memory in order, God's mercy named at every failure, sin named without excuses: that is the book's method, because it is the prayer's. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
