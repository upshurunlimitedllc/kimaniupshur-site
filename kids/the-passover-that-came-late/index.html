The Passover That Came Late

2 Chronicles 30 and the Invitation Some Mocked and Some Obeyed

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

The Passover was a month late, the messengers were laughed at in the streets, and many who came to the feast were not even cleansed — and it became one of the greatest gatherings in Israel's history.

The Passover That Came Late: 2 Chronicles 30 and the Invitation Some Mocked and Some Obeyed stays inside 2 Chronicles 30 across ten chapters, and tells only Hezekiah's Passover — the king as inviter and intercessor, gathering a divided people (the Exodus 12 institution is a different feast, told in its own book). Children watch the letters go out to all Israel, the counsel to keep the feast in the second month because neither priests nor people were ready in the first, and the posts carrying the invitation from Beer-sheba to Dan. Some hearers “laughed them to scorn, and mocked them” — nevertheless, some humbled themselves and came, Judah was given one heart, and a very great congregation gathered at Jerusalem. Then the centre of the chapter: many ate the Passover otherwise than it was written, and the king prayed for them openly — “The good LORD pardon every one that prepareth his heart to seek God” — and “the LORD hearkened to Hezekiah, and healed the people.” The feast ran seven days, and then, in joy, seven more, until the priests' blessing rose from the city and was heard in heaven. For any child who has ever felt too late, too unprepared, or from too far away, this book's answer is the chapter's own: come anyway — the table is still set. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
