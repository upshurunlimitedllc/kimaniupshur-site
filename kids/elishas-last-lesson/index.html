Elisha's Last Lesson

2 Kings 13 and the Arrows the King Stopped Striking

by Kimani Upshur, M.Ed.

For Children • Ages 6–12

A dying prophet, a weeping king, a bow, an open window — and a handful of arrows struck against a stone floor. Everything in this book happens in one room, and everything in the room counts.

Elisha's Last Lesson: 2 Kings 13 and the Arrows the King Stopped Striking stays inside 2 Kings 13:14–19 across ten chapters, and tells only what those six verses record. It begins in one plain sentence: "Now Elisha was fallen sick of his sickness whereof he died." King Joash comes down to the quiet room, weeps over the old man's face, and cries out to him as a father; then, at the prophet's word, he takes bow and arrows, feels Elisha's hands laid over his own, opens the window eastward, and shoots "the arrow of the LORD's deliverance." Last comes the command with no number in it — "Smite upon the ground" — and the counted answer: "And he smote thrice, and stayed." The dying prophet's anger, and his word, close the lesson: "Thou shouldest have smitten five or six times." Why the king stopped at three, the Bible never says, and this book never invents it — no motives are furnished for any heart God left unnarrated. The application is drawn only from what is recorded: the counted response, and what our own countable responses to God's word look like — tasks finished fully, obedience carried all the way to the last strike. Every chapter ends with a Do and a Pray to share together. Every Scripture quotation is word-for-word from the King James Version (KJV). The book closes with a special invitation for children to give their lives to Jesus. For older children ages 9-12.

Written from a Christian worldview — Scripture first.
